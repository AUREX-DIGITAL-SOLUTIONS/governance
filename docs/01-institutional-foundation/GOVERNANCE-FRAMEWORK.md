# Governance Framework

## Aurex Digital Solutions

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft v1.0  
**Institution:** Aurex Digital Solutions  
**Document type:** Governance Framework
**Purpose:** Define how authority, accountability, decisions, oversight, and escalation operate inside Aurex
**Phase:** Founding / pre-formalization

> This framework governs internal authority and accountability; it does not create authority over external institutions or infrastructure.

## 1. Governance Objective

Aurex requires governance that is explicit, proportionate, evidence-driven, technically competent, safety-aware, and capable of maturing beyond founder dependence.

The governing rule is:

> Authority must be explicit, limited to a defined scope, and matched with accountability.

Technical capability does not create legal, regulatory, ownership, operational, or market authority. The [Organization Charter](ORGANIZATION-CHARTER.md) establishes the constitutional boundary; this framework defines how Aurex makes and reviews decisions within it.

## 2. Governance Architecture

Aurex should use five connected governance functions:

```text
Governing oversight
        -> Executive execution
        -> Technical governance
        -> Programme governance
        -> Assurance and review
        -> Delivery teams
```

At the founding stage, one person may hold several functions temporarily. The functions should still be defined separately so that temporary founder concentration does not become the permanent model.

### Governing oversight

A future board, council, member body, or equivalent legal body provides institutional oversight, protects the mission, appoints or oversees executive leadership, reviews major commitments, and holds the institution accountable. It should not be invented before it formally exists.

### Founder authority

During formation, the founder may provide transitional authority for institutional direction, foundational documents, programme initiation, initial partnerships, resource allocation, technical direction, and public representation. This authority is limited, documented, and intended to transfer progressively into defined institutional roles.

### Executive function

Executive leadership translates approved direction into strategy execution, people, budgets, operations, partnerships, risk implementation, and reporting. Titles should be created only when workload and accountability justify them.

### Technical governance

Technical governance protects architecture, engineering quality, interoperability, cybersecurity, data, safety, technical claims, and deployment readiness. The detailed controls are in the [Security & Responsible Technology Policy](../02-technical-governance/SECURITY-AND-RESPONSIBLE-TECHNOLOGY-POLICY.md) and [Open-Source & Licensing Framework](../02-technical-governance/OPEN-SOURCE-AND-LICENSING-FRAMEWORK.md).

### Programme governance

Programme governance ensures that significant programmes have an owner, purpose, scope, resources, milestones, risks, evidence requirements, decision gates, reporting, and closure criteria. The [Programme Governance Framework](../03-operations-and-development/PROGRAMME-GOVERNANCE-FRAMEWORK.md) defines the lifecycle in detail.

### Assurance

Assurance provides proportionate challenge for safety, cybersecurity, legal, financial, research, public-interest, and readiness risks. The people delivering work should not always be the only people deciding whether it is safe or ready.

## 3. Interim Governance

Until formal governance bodies exist:

1. The founder holds transitional institutional authority.
2. Material strategic, financial, technical, legal, external, and safety-related decisions are documented.
3. Safety, law, security, and public-interest obligations override speed or preference.
4. Conflicts of interest are disclosed and managed.
5. Programme ownership is explicit once a programme has meaningful scope or risk.
6. Permissions and access follow roles rather than personal convenience.
7. High-risk actions receive additional review where practical.
8. This framework evolves as participation, resources, and institutional risk increase.

Founder authority does not extend to external institutions, government bodies, utilities, partners, customers, asset owners, or public infrastructure without appropriate authority.

## 4. Decision Classes

Different decisions require different levels of review.

| Class | Description | Typical authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operational | Routine and reversible work | Delegated role or team |
| Material | Meaningful resource, technical, legal, safety, or reputational impact | Founder, executive, or accountable role within limits |
| Strategic | Institutional direction, major programme, country entry, funding model, or major partnership | Founder or executive leadership with governing oversight when available |
| Constitutional | Mission, Charter, control, ownership, dissolution, or fundamental governance | Highest authorized level; transitional founder authority until formal governance exists |

Every significant decision should identify:

```text
Who proposes?
Who reviews?
Who decides?
Who executes?
Who must be informed?
```

[Organization Structure & Decision Rights](ORGANIZATION-STRUCTURE-AND-DECISION-RIGHTS.md) converts this model into roles, reporting lines, and decision matrices. RACI may clarify workflow, but it does not create legal or institutional authority.

## 5. Decision Standard

Significant decisions should follow a proportionate path:

```text
Problem -> Requirements -> Options -> Evidence -> Decision -> Implementation -> Review
```

A decision record should normally include the question, decision, date, decision-maker, authority, evidence, alternatives, rationale, risks, conditions, and review date. Routine low-risk work does not require heavy bureaucracy.

Aurex should scale systems and claims only when evidence, governance, cybersecurity maturity, institutional trust, and appropriate authority support the next stage.

## 6. Delegation

Authority may be delegated only when the delegation records:

- delegating authority and recipient;
- scope and decision limits;
- financial limits, where relevant;
- duration;
- reporting requirements; and
- revocation conditions.

Delegated authority cannot exceed the authority of the delegating person or body. Decision rights should attach to roles, not personalities, wherever possible.

[Organization Structure & Decision Rights](ORGANIZATION-STRUCTURE-AND-DECISION-RIGHTS.md) defines how delegation is recorded and applied to roles.

## 7. Programme and Technical Gates

A significant programme or technical initiative should pass through proportionate gates:

```text
Problem -> Research -> Proposal -> Review -> Experiment -> Validation -> Pilot -> Deployment -> Evaluation
```

Progression is not automatic. Each stage should state its maturity, evidence, risks, dependencies, authority, and conditions. The [Research & Innovation Framework](../03-operations-and-development/RESEARCH-AND-INNOVATION-FRAMEWORK.md), [Operating Model](../03-operations-and-development/OPERATING-MODEL.md), and [Programme Governance Framework](../03-operations-and-development/PROGRAMME-GOVERNANCE-FRAMEWORK.md) provide supporting requirements.

## 8. Escalation, Stop-Work, and Emergencies

Concerns should be escalated when they exceed delegated authority or involve:

- human or electrical safety;
- cybersecurity or data risk;
- legal or regulatory uncertainty;
- unauthorized access or control;
- material budget or schedule deviation;
- conflict of interest;
- significant technical failure;
- public, partner, or community harm;
- unresolved authority disputes.

Anyone involved in technical, field, security, data, or infrastructure-related work may raise a good-faith stop-work concern. It must receive prompt review without retaliation.

Designated roles may take immediate protective action during an emergency to protect people, equipment, data, systems, or essential operations. Emergency action must be documented and reviewed afterward; it is not a permanent bypass of governance and does not authorize unlawful external control.

Detailed behavioral and non-retaliation requirements are in the [Code of Conduct](../../CODE-OF-CONDUCT.md).

[Organization Structure & Decision Rights](ORGANIZATION-STRUCTURE-AND-DECISION-RIGHTS.md) applies these standards to role-based decisions.

## 9. Conflicts, Partnerships, and Funding

People involved in decisions must disclose actual, potential, or perceived conflicts. Aurex may require recusal, access limits, independent review, or disclosure.

Major partnerships should record purpose, scope, owner, legal basis, financial and data terms, intellectual-property terms, authority boundaries, communications rules, and exit provisions. Discussion, endorsement, formal partnership, legal authority, and operational permission must not be conflated.

Funding may support research and execution but must not predetermine technical conclusions. Material restrictions, influence, and public-interest risks should be recorded.

Detailed requirements are maintained in the [Partnership & Stakeholder Framework](../03-operations-and-development/PARTNERSHIP-AND-STAKEHOLDER-FRAMEWORK.md), [Funding & Financial Framework](../03-operations-and-development/FUNDING-AND-FINANCIAL-FRAMEWORK.md), and [Research & Innovation Framework](../03-operations-and-development/RESEARCH-AND-INNOVATION-FRAMEWORK.md).

## 10. Transparency and Records

Aurex should maintain retrievable, versioned records for material decisions, delegations, policies, risks, programmes, partnerships, funding commitments, technical decisions, research, and Charter changes.

It should disclose appropriate information about governance, programme status, public research, open-source work, and major decisions while protecting privacy, cybersecurity, legal restrictions, contractual confidentiality, and sensitive infrastructure information.

Transparency must describe what exists without implying formal maturity, authorization, or endorsement that has not been established.

## 11. Governance Maturity

Aurex should mature through these stages:

```text
Founder-led -> Documented -> Delegated roles -> Independent assurance -> Institutional governance
```

The present model is founder-led and moving toward documented governance. Progress should be based on real participation, resources, risk, and workload rather than organizational appearance.

Governance performance should be reviewed by asking whether authority is clear, decisions are timely and traceable, risks are escalated, programmes are accountable, and the founder is no longer an unnecessary bottleneck.
