# Task 16 - Funding & Financial Framework
## Aurex Digital Solutions

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Foundational Draft / Draft v1.0  
**Institution:** Aurex Digital Solutions  
**Phase:** External / Institutional Development  
**Scope:** Funding strategy, financial governance, resource stewardship and institutional sustainability  
**Navigation:** [Previous: Organization Roadmap](./ORGANIZATION-ROADMAP.md) | [Home: Documentation Index](./README.md) | [Next: Partnership & Stakeholder Framework](./PARTNERSHIP-AND-STAKEHOLDER-FRAMEWORK.md)

Task 15 established **where Aurex intends to go and the evidence gates required to advance**.

Task 16 answers:

> **How does Aurex finance that journey without compromising its mission, institutional independence, technical integrity or financial sustainability?**

The governing principle is:

> **Funding enables the mission. It does not purchase institutional truth.**

---

## 1. Purpose

The Funding & Financial Framework establishes how Aurex should:

- obtain financial resources;
- evaluate funding opportunities;
- allocate resources;
- authorize expenditure;
- maintain financial records;
- manage financial risk;
- protect institutional independence;
- fund research and technology development;
- finance programmes;
- manage restricted funding;
- account for assets;
- develop sustainable revenue;
- prepare for external financial accountability.

Aurex should treat money as **institutional infrastructure**, not simply capital to spend.

---

## 2. Financial Mission

Aurex's financial system exists to ensure that sufficient resources are available to advance its mission responsibly.

Conceptually:

```text
MISSION
   ↓
STRATEGY
   ↓
ROADMAP
   ↓
RESOURCE REQUIREMENTS
   ↓
FUNDING
   ↓
ALLOCATION
   ↓
EXECUTION
   ↓
EVIDENCE / OUTCOMES
   ↓
ACCOUNTABILITY
```

Funding should follow strategy.

Strategy should not be rewritten merely to follow available money.

---

## 3. Financial Principles

Aurex adopts the following foundational principles:

1. **Mission before money**
2. **Institutional independence**
3. **Financial integrity**
4. **Traceability**
5. **Proportional controls**
6. **Value for money**
7. **Sustainability**
8. **Diversification**
9. **Evidence before scale**
10. **No financial authority without accountability**

These principles are mandatory even when Aurex is small.

Their application should be proportional to actual activity, financial materiality, legal structure, funding conditions and institutional maturity.

---

## 4. Mission Before Money

A funding opportunity is not automatically a good opportunity.

Before accepting material funding, Aurex should ask:

```text
DOES IT SUPPORT THE MISSION?
        ↓
DOES IT DISTORT THE MISSION?
        ↓
WHAT OBLIGATIONS COME WITH IT?
        ↓
WHAT CONTROL DOES THE FUNDER RECEIVE?
        ↓
WHAT DEPENDENCY DOES IT CREATE?
        ↓
CAN AUREX DELIVER RESPONSIBLY?
```

Money that materially compromises Aurex's purpose may cost more institutionally than it contributes financially.

---

## 5. Institutional Independence

Funders may legitimately:

- define grant conditions;
- negotiate investment terms;
- require reporting;
- define contracted deliverables;
- audit use of restricted funds;
- participate in agreed governance where legally established.

They should not automatically gain authority to:

- falsify research conclusions;
- suppress legitimate technical findings;
- bypass safety;
- manipulate evidence;
- claim nonexistent project maturity;
- direct unauthorized infrastructure control.

Aurex maintains:

> **Funding does not purchase institutional truth.**

---

## 6. Financial Integrity

Aurex should maintain accurate records of:

- money received;
- money spent;
- obligations;
- assets;
- liabilities;
- restrictions;
- programme expenditure;
- material commitments.

Financial records should represent reality.

Aurex should not use financial documentation to imply that revenue, grants, bank accounts, investors, budgets, financial statements, approvals, legal structures, departments or audit status exist unless they actually exist.

---

## 7. Financial Maturity F0-F4

Aurex's financial system should grow with the institution.

```text
F0 - FOUNDING
Founder-managed / minimal activity
        ↓
F1 - DOCUMENTED
Budgets, records and controls
        ↓
F2 - CONTROLLED
Delegated authority and structured accounting
        ↓
F3 - ASSURED
Independent review / stronger financial controls
        ↓
F4 - INSTITUTIONAL
Mature finance capability and external accountability
```

Aurex should build the financial system required for its **actual stage**, not imitate a large corporation prematurely.

---

## 8. Current Financial Reality

At the founding stage, Aurex should not invent:

- a CFO;
- finance department;
- investment committee;
- audit committee;
- large budgets;
- revenue;
- grants;
- investors;
- donors;
- bank accounts;
- audited financial statements;
- public funding;
- legal capacity to issue equity;
- formal approval committees.

unless they actually exist.

Current documentation should therefore establish **financial rules before financial complexity emerges**.

Founder-led financial control, key-person concentration and concentration of financial functions should be documented honestly as current-stage control limitations, not hidden by fictional roles.

---

## 9. Funding Architecture

Aurex may eventually use multiple funding mechanisms:

```text
FOUNDING CAPITAL
      +
GRANTS
      +
RESEARCH FUNDING
      +
PHILANTHROPIC / DEVELOPMENT FUNDING
      +
STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIP FUNDING
      +
COMMERCIAL REVENUE
      +
INVESTMENT CAPITAL
      +
PUBLIC / DEVELOPMENT FINANCE
      +
OTHER LEGITIMATE SOURCES
      ↓
SUSTAINABLE INSTITUTION
```

Not every mechanism is appropriate at every stage.

This framework describes possible future funding mechanisms. It does not state that any particular source has been obtained, committed, approved or validated.

---

## 10. Founder Funding

Early Aurex activity may be supported through founder resources.

Where founder money materially funds institutional activity, records should progressively distinguish:

```text
PERSONAL EXPENDITURE
        ≠
INSTITUTIONAL EXPENDITURE
```

Once Aurex has an appropriate legal/financial structure, institutional finances should be separated from personal finances.

Founder funding should not remove the need for records, accountability, conflict management or realistic assessment of sustainability.

---

## 11. Grants

Grants may be particularly appropriate for:

- research;
- open-source development;
- public-interest infrastructure;
- feasibility studies;
- capability building;
- prototypes;
- pilots;
- innovation programmes.

Before accepting a grant, Aurex should understand:

- permitted use;
- restricted activities;
- milestones;
- reporting;
- IP terms;
- data obligations;
- procurement rules;
- audit rights;
- repayment/clawback conditions;
- publicity obligations.

A grant should not be treated as free money. It is money with purpose, conditions and accountability.

---

## 12. Research Funding

Research funding should not determine research conclusions.

Aurex should distinguish:

```text
FUNDER DEFINES RESEARCH QUESTION
              ✓

FUNDER PURCHASES DESIRED ANSWER
              ✗
```

Funding sources and material conflicts should be disclosed where appropriate.

Negative findings, failed hypotheses and inconvenient evidence remain valid research outputs.

---

## 13. Philanthropic and Development Funding

Mission-aligned philanthropic or development funding may support:

- energy access;
- interoperability;
- African technical capability;
- open-source infrastructure;
- research;
- public-interest programmes.

Such funding should still undergo:

- mission review;
- dependency review;
- conditions review;
- financial review;
- sovereignty/public-interest review.

Good intentions do not eliminate governance requirements.

---

## 14. Strategic Partnership Funding

Partners may contribute:

- money;
- equipment;
- cloud resources;
- technical expertise;
- facilities;
- data access;
- engineering support.

These are resources even when cash does not change hands.

Aurex should therefore recognize **in-kind contributions** where material.

In-kind contributions should be tracked when they materially affect capability, dependency, valuation, reporting obligations, intellectual property, operational continuity or partner influence.

---

## 15. Commercial Revenue

Long-term sustainability may require earned revenue.

Potential revenue models should emerge from validated use cases rather than be invented solely for this framework.

Possible categories could eventually include:

- software/services;
- integration;
- infrastructure management;
- technical support;
- research;
- engineering;
- programme delivery;
- training;
- other mission-aligned services.

This framework does **not** establish that any particular model has been commercially validated.

---

## 16. Revenue Principle

Revenue should ideally reinforce Aurex's mission.

```text
MISSION
   ↕
VALUE CREATED
   ↕
VALUE CAPTURED
```

A business model that generates revenue while undermining interoperability, public interest or institutional integrity should be questioned.

Revenue should not depend on misleading maturity claims, vendor lock-in that contradicts Aurex's public-interest posture, unsafe operational shortcuts or improper influence over research conclusions.

---

## 17. Equity Capital

Equity investment may eventually be appropriate if Aurex adopts a legal and organizational structure capable of issuing equity.

Before equity financing, Aurex should understand:

- valuation;
- dilution;
- investor rights;
- governance rights;
- liquidation terms;
- information rights;
- control implications;
- future financing effects;
- mission implications.

Task 16 does not assume Aurex currently has equity available to issue.

---

## 18. Debt

Debt creates repayment obligations.

Before accepting debt, Aurex should assess:

```text
REPAYMENT SOURCE
+
CASH FLOW
+
INTEREST
+
COLLATERAL
+
COVENANTS
+
DEFAULT CONSEQUENCES
```

Debt should not be used merely because other capital is unavailable.

Debt may be inappropriate where revenue, reserves, legal capacity, repayment discipline or institutional controls are not yet mature enough to carry the obligation.

---

## 19. Public Funding

Government or publicly funded programmes may eventually be relevant.

Aurex must not assume entitlement to public funding.

Any such funding should follow applicable:

- procurement;
- contracting;
- reporting;
- audit;
- public-finance;
- anti-corruption

requirements.

Public funding should not be described as committed, available, approved or expected unless there is actual evidence of that status.

---

Aurex's roadmap suggests different resource needs at different stages.

### Foundation

Likely needs:

- research;
- documentation;
- basic infrastructure;
- legal preparation;
- technical experimentation.

### Validation

Funding supports:

- research;
- stakeholder discovery;
- technical studies;
- equipment access.

### Prototype

Funding supports:

- hardware;
- engineering;
- testing;
- infrastructure;
- cybersecurity.

### Pilot

Funding supports:

- deployment;
- installation;
- field engineering;
- monitoring;
- evaluation;
- insurance/compliance where applicable.

### Programme

Funding supports:

- teams;
- operations;
- programme management;
- support;
- assurance.

### Scale

Funding requirements may become infrastructure-scale and institutionally complex.

The amount and form of capital should evolve with evidence and institutional capability.

---

## 21. Evidence-Linked Funding

Aurex should connect capital requirements to evidence.

```text
FUNDING
   ↓
CAPABILITY
   ↓
EXPERIMENT / WORK
   ↓
EVIDENCE
   ↓
DECISION
   ↓
NEXT FUNDING STAGE
```

This reduces premature capital deployment.

Evidence-linked funding means Aurex should avoid raising, accepting or spending capital at a scale that exceeds its ability to govern the work, validate outcomes or manage risk.

---

## 22. Funding Request Principle

Aurex should increasingly be able to answer:

> **What exactly will this funding allow us to prove, build, operate or sustain?**

A funding request without a defined outcome is difficult to govern.

Funding requests should identify purpose, expected outcome, evidence to be produced, risk, restrictions, accountability and sustainability implications.

---

## 23. Restricted Funding

Some funding may only be used for specified purposes.

Restricted resources should be identifiable separately from unrestricted institutional resources.

```text
RESTRICTED FUND
      ↓
DEFINED PURPOSE
      ↓
ELIGIBLE EXPENDITURE
      ↓
TRACKING
      ↓
REPORTING
```

Restricted funds should not silently subsidize unrelated activity.

Misuse of restricted funds creates financial, legal, reputational and institutional risk.

---

## 24. Unrestricted Funding

Unrestricted resources provide institutional flexibility.

They may support:

- core operations;
- research;
- staffing;
- infrastructure;
- strategic priorities;
- reserves.

Even unrestricted money remains subject to Aurex's financial controls.

Unrestricted funding should not be confused with unaccountable funding.

---

## 25. Funding Concentration Risk

Aurex should monitor dependence on:

- one donor;
- one investor;
- one customer;
- one government programme;
- one commercial partner.

High concentration can create:

- financial fragility;
- negotiating weakness;
- mission pressure;
- operational instability.

Diversification should therefore become a long-term resilience objective.

---

## 26. Funding Dependency Test

For material funding:

```text
IF THIS FUNDER DISAPPEARS,
WHAT STOPS?
```

The answer reveals dependency.

Aurex should know the consequences before accepting concentration.

Dependency is not automatically disqualifying, but it must be understood, recorded and governed.

---

## 27. Budgeting

A budget is a resource plan tied to objectives.

It should answer:

```text
WHAT ARE WE TRYING TO ACHIEVE?
WHAT WILL IT COST?
WHEN IS THE RESOURCE NEEDED?
WHO OWNS THE BUDGET?
WHAT ASSUMPTIONS EXIST?
WHAT HAPPENS IF COST CHANGES?
```

Budgeting should follow the roadmap.

Aurex should not invent budgets before actual activities, commitments and financial scale justify them.

---

## 28. Budget Categories

As Aurex develops, budgets may distinguish:

- institutional operations;
- research;
- engineering;
- programme delivery;
- equipment;
- infrastructure;
- people;
- professional services;
- travel;
- security;
- contingency.

Categories should reflect actual activity.

Budget categories should help management and accountability rather than create false precision.

---

## 29. Budget Ownership

Every material budget should eventually have one accountable owner.

The owner is responsible for:

- planning;
- monitoring;
- explaining variance;
- ensuring appropriate expenditure.

Budget ownership does not automatically grant unlimited spending authority.

At the current founder-led stage, ownership and authorization may be concentrated, but that concentration should be recognized as a control limitation.

---

## 30. Financial Authorization

Expenditure requires authority.

Aurex should distinguish:

```text
REQUEST
   ≠
APPROVAL
   ≠
PAYMENT
   ≠
ACCOUNTING
```

As the institution grows, these functions should be separated where practical.

No financial authority should exist without accountability.

---

## 31. Current Authorization Model

At the current founder-led stage, multiple financial functions may necessarily be concentrated.

That concentration should be treated as a **known control limitation and key-person risk**.

As activity grows, financial responsibilities should progressively separate.

Aurex should not disguise this stage by inventing committees, officers or departments that do not yet exist.

---

## 32. Financial Thresholds

Aurex should **not invent monetary approval thresholds before actual financial scale and governance requirements are known**.

Instead:

> **Approval authority should initially scale with materiality, risk, contractual obligation and available delegated authority.**

Specific thresholds can later be formally approved.

Until thresholds exist, material expenditure should be evaluated through purpose, risk, affordability, records, conflicts, obligations and authority.

---

## 33. Expenditure Test

Before material expenditure:

```text
IS IT AUTHORIZED?
IS IT BUDGETED?
IS IT NECESSARY?
IS THE PRICE REASONABLE?
IS THE SUPPLIER APPROPRIATE?
ARE CONFLICTS DISCLOSED?
WHAT RISK DOES IT CREATE?
IS THERE A RECORD?
```

If a material expense cannot pass this test, it should be delayed, reduced, escalated or rejected.

---

## 34. Value for Money

Lowest cost does not always equal best value.

Aurex should consider:

```text
PURCHASE PRICE
+
OPERATING COST
+
MAINTENANCE
+
INTEGRATION
+
SECURITY
+
LOCK-IN
+
REPLACEMENT
+
END-OF-LIFE
```

This is especially important for infrastructure technology.

Value for money should include lifecycle cost, technical suitability, maintainability, security, interoperability, dependency and exit options.

---

## 35. Procurement Relationship

Task 16 establishes financial principles around procurement.

Detailed procurement procedures should be developed when procurement activity justifies them.

Material procurement should consider:

- competition where appropriate;
- technical fitness;
- lifecycle cost;
- conflicts;
- security;
- interoperability;
- supplier dependency.

Procurement choices should connect to Task 12's sovereignty and dependency principles and Task 13's risk discipline.

---

## 36. Financial Records

Aurex should maintain records sufficient to reconstruct material transactions.

Records may include:

- invoices;
- receipts;
- contracts;
- purchase approvals;
- payment records;
- bank records;
- grant documentation;
- asset records.

Retention requirements should eventually follow applicable law and contractual obligations.

A record does not need to be complex at the founding stage, but it should be truthful, retrievable and tied to the purpose of the transaction.

---

## 37. Institutional Accounts

Once legally and operationally appropriate, Aurex should use financial accounts controlled in the institution's name rather than relying indefinitely on personal financial channels.

Access should follow:

- least privilege;
- documented authority;
- secure authentication;
- appropriate oversight.

This framework does not claim that institutional bank accounts currently exist.

---

## 38. Accounting

As financial activity becomes material, Aurex should establish an appropriate accounting system capable of tracking:

- income;
- expenditure;
- assets;
- liabilities;
- restricted funding;
- programme costs.

Professional accounting support should be added when complexity or legal obligations justify it.

Accounting systems should reflect actual legal structure, reporting obligations and operational maturity.

---

## 39. Financial Reporting

Financial reporting should answer:

1. What resources does Aurex have?
2. Where did they come from?
3. What restrictions apply?
4. What has been spent?
5. Against which objective?
6. What commitments remain?
7. What financial risks exist?
8. How long can operations continue?

Reports should be proportionate to audience, risk and obligation.

---

## 40. Financial Transparency

Transparency should be appropriate to audience and obligation.

Aurex may need different reporting for:

- internal governance;
- funders;
- investors;
- regulators;
- tax authorities;
- programme partners;
- the public.

Transparency does not mean publishing confidential financial information indiscriminately.

Public transparency should be accurate and should not imply funding, revenue, approvals or assurance that do not exist.

---

## 41. Cash Flow

Profitability and cash availability are not the same.

Aurex should monitor:

```text
CASH AVAILABLE
      ↓
EXPECTED INFLOWS
      ↓
COMMITTED OUTFLOWS
      ↓
OPERATING NEEDS
```

A technically successful organization can still fail if it cannot meet obligations when due.

Cash-flow planning should become more formal as recurring obligations emerge.

---

## 42. Runway

As recurring operating costs emerge, Aurex should monitor runway.

Conceptually:

```text
AVAILABLE OPERATING CASH
÷
EXPECTED PERIODIC NET CASH USE
≈
RUNWAY
```

This is a planning estimate, not certainty.

Runway should not be overstated by counting uncertain funding, conditional grants, unpaid pledges or speculative revenue as available cash.

---

## 43. Reserves

As Aurex matures, maintaining financial reserves may protect against:

- delayed funding;
- customer loss;
- emergency repairs;
- infrastructure failures;
- unexpected obligations.

Reserve policy should emerge from actual operating exposure.

A reserve should be purposeful, governed and distinguishable from restricted funds where restrictions apply.

---

## 44. Contingency

Material budgets may include contingency for uncertainty.

Contingency is not unaccountable spending.

Its use should remain documented and authorized.

Contingency should be tied to realistic uncertainty such as technical integration, field deployment, currency movement, equipment cost, compliance, travel or supplier availability.

---

## 45. Programme Accounting

Programmes should progressively distinguish:

```text
PROGRAMME FUNDING
       ↓
PROGRAMME BUDGET
       ↓
PROGRAMME EXPENDITURE
       ↓
PROGRAMME OUTPUT
       ↓
PROGRAMME OUTCOME
```

This enables evaluation of whether resources produced intended value.

Programme accounting becomes especially important when funds are restricted, co-funded, in-kind, partner-provided or tied to defined milestones.

---

## 46. Cost Attribution

Where multiple programmes share infrastructure or personnel, Aurex should progressively establish reasonable cost-attribution methods.

False precision is unnecessary.

Consistency and transparency matter.

Cost attribution should be documented well enough that stakeholders can understand how shared costs were assigned.

---

## 47. Asset Management

Material assets may include:

- computers;
- test equipment;
- energy hardware;
- networking equipment;
- software licenses;
- laboratory equipment.

Aurex should know:

```text
WHAT DO WE OWN?
WHERE IS IT?
WHO IS RESPONSIBLE?
WHAT IS ITS CONDITION?
WHAT IS ITS VALUE?
WHEN SHOULD IT BE REPLACED?
```

Asset records should strengthen as assets become material, portable, safety-relevant, restricted-funded or mission-critical.

---

## 48. Intellectual Property as an Asset

Software, documentation, research and designs may carry intellectual value.

Task 9 governs open-source licensing.

Financial treatment of intellectual property should not contradict Aurex's open-source commitments.

> **Openly licensed does not mean economically valueless.**

Aurex should preserve clarity around ownership, licensing, contributor rights, commercialization boundaries and public-interest commitments.

---

## 49. Liabilities and Commitments

Aurex should track not only money already spent but commitments such as:

- contracts;
- subscriptions;
- debt;
- lease obligations;
- grant obligations;
- warranties;
- programme commitments.

A future obligation can be as important as a current expense.

Commitments should be accepted only by people with authority to bind the institution.

---

## 50. Financial Risk

Financial risks should integrate with [Task 13 - Risk Management Framework](./RISK-MANAGEMENT-FRAMEWORK.md).

Examples:

- insufficient funding;
- cost overruns;
- cash-flow shortfalls;
- currency exposure;
- donor concentration;
- customer concentration;
- fraud;
- asset loss;
- unplanned liabilities.

Material financial risks belong in the institutional risk register.

Financial risk should be treated as institutional risk, not merely accounting detail.

---

## 51. Currency Risk

International funding or procurement may expose Aurex to exchange-rate movements.

Where material, budgeting should distinguish the currency in which:

- funding is received;
- costs are incurred;
- obligations are denominated.

Aurex should not assume currency stability.

Currency exposure should be considered before accepting foreign-currency obligations or relying on foreign-currency funding to cover local costs.

---

## 52. Fraud Risk

Financial systems should progressively reduce opportunities for:

- false invoices;
- unauthorized payments;
- expense manipulation;
- theft;
- duplicate payment;
- procurement collusion;
- misuse of restricted funds.

Controls should strengthen with financial scale.

Early controls may be simple, but they should still create traceability and accountability.

---

## 53. Anti-Bribery and Corruption

Aurex should not offer, request, authorize or knowingly facilitate bribery or improper financial inducements.

This applies particularly to:

- procurement;
- government engagement;
- licensing;
- partnerships;
- funding;
- contracting.

The [Code of Conduct](./CODE-OF-CONDUCT.md) remains applicable.

Aurex should not treat bribery or improper inducement as a cost of doing business.

---

## 54. Gifts and Hospitality

Material gifts or hospitality can create actual or perceived conflicts.

Aurex should eventually define detailed thresholds if activity requires them.

Until then:

> Gifts or hospitality must never purchase decisions, access, favorable technical conclusions or improper institutional influence.

Gifts and hospitality should be declined, disclosed or recorded where necessary to protect institutional integrity.

---

## 55. Conflicts of Interest

Anyone participating in material financial decisions should disclose relevant conflicts.

Examples:

- ownership in a supplier;
- family relationship;
- personal financial interest;
- undisclosed commission.

Disclosure does not automatically mean wrongdoing.

It enables appropriate management.

---

## 56. Related-Party Transactions

Transactions involving founders, leadership, staff, contributors, advisers, family members, controlled companies or closely related parties should be handled carefully.

A related-party transaction may be legitimate, but it should be:

- disclosed;
- documented;
- justified;
- priced reasonably;
- authorized appropriately;
- managed for conflict risk.

Aurex should not use related-party arrangements to bypass procurement, accountability, grant restrictions, investor obligations or public-interest commitments.

---

## 57. Funder Influence

Funders may influence scope through legitimate funding terms.

They should not improperly influence:

- evidence;
- safety conclusions;
- research results;
- public claims;
- risk acceptance;
- regulatory statements;
- infrastructure-control authority.

Aurex should document material funder conditions before accepting funding.

---

## 58. Investor Influence

Investors may receive rights if those rights are validly agreed through an appropriate legal structure.

Investor influence should be assessed for:

- mission alignment;
- governance implications;
- control rights;
- exit pressure;
- public-interest tension;
- open-source compatibility;
- long-term institutional independence.

Task 16 does not assume that Aurex currently has investors or has issued equity.

---

## 59. Financial Independence

Financial independence does not mean refusing all external funding.

It means Aurex retains enough institutional judgment, diversification, evidence discipline and governance control to avoid having its mission or truth purchased by a single source of money.

Financial independence is strengthened by:

- diversified funding;
- unrestricted reserves where possible;
- clear restrictions;
- transparent records;
- conflict controls;
- realistic commitments;
- evidence-linked growth.

---

## 60. Funding and Sovereignty

Funding can create dependency.

Aurex should review material funding for sovereignty and dependency implications, including:

- foreign control over critical knowledge;
- restrictions on local capability building;
- data-hosting or data-access requirements;
- vendor lock-in;
- exclusive partnerships;
- conditions that weaken public-interest obligations.

This connects directly to [Task 12 - Ethics, Sovereignty & Public-Interest Framework](./ETHICS-SOVEREIGNTY-AND-PUBLIC-INTEREST-FRAMEWORK.md).

---

## 61. Funding and Open Source

Aurex's open-source posture does not remove the need for sustainable funding.

Funding for open-source work may support:

- maintainers;
- security review;
- documentation;
- testing;
- community support;
- interoperability work;
- responsible release processes.

Financial sustainability should reinforce the open-source strategy defined in [Task 9 - Open-Source & Licensing Framework](./OPEN-SOURCE-AND-LICENSING-FRAMEWORK.md).

Aurex should avoid funding arrangements that require abandoning open-source commitments without explicit institutional review.

---

## 62. Funding and Research Integrity

Research integrity requires that financial support be visible enough to assess bias and conflicts.

Aurex should protect:

- method integrity;
- data integrity;
- conclusion integrity;
- publication integrity where applicable;
- disclosure of material funder interest.

Funding may support research activity.

It should not purchase predetermined conclusions.

---

## 63. Financial Evidence Gate

Before advancing financially, Aurex should ask:

```text
WHAT MONEY IS REQUIRED?
WHAT EVIDENCE JUSTIFIES IT?
WHAT CONTROL SYSTEM EXISTS?
WHAT OBLIGATION WILL IT CREATE?
WHAT HAPPENS IF THE ASSUMPTION FAILS?
```

A financial evidence gate protects Aurex from scaling obligations faster than capability.

---

## 64. Funding Gate by Programme Maturity

Funding should match programme maturity.

| Programme maturity | Appropriate financial posture |
|---|---|
| Foundation | Low-complexity resources for research, documentation and basic capability |
| Validation | Targeted funding tied to specific questions and evidence |
| Prototype | Controlled funding for build, test, security and technical validation |
| Pilot | Restricted and monitored funding tied to defined deployment outcomes |
| Programme | Structured budgets, reporting, risk ownership and programme accounting |
| Scale | Institutional finance capability, assurance, reserves and external accountability |

Aurex should not seek scale-stage capital for foundation-stage evidence unless the purpose is clearly capability building and controls are adequate.

---

## 65. Financial Stop Conditions

Aurex should be prepared to pause, reduce scope or reject funding where:

- conditions distort the mission;
- funding requires false maturity claims;
- obligations exceed capacity;
- restrictions cannot be tracked;
- repayment cannot be supported;
- conflicts cannot be managed;
- cybersecurity, safety or legal risks are unacceptable;
- funder influence threatens institutional truth.

Stopping or declining funding may be the financially responsible decision.

---

## 66. Financial Sustainability

Financial sustainability means Aurex can continue mission-relevant work without depending on fragile, opaque or mission-distorting resource flows.

Sustainability may eventually require a mix of:

- grants;
- earned revenue;
- partnerships;
- research funding;
- philanthropic/development support;
- reserves;
- appropriate investment or debt if legally and institutionally suitable.

The correct mix should emerge from evidence, legal structure, market validation, programme needs and institutional capability.

---

## 67. Unit Economics

Where Aurex develops repeatable services, products or programmes, it should eventually understand unit economics.

Questions may include:

```text
WHAT DOES ONE DEPLOYMENT COST?
WHAT DOES ONE CUSTOMER OR PROGRAMME COST TO SERVE?
WHAT SUPPORT BURDEN IS CREATED?
WHAT REVENUE OR PUBLIC VALUE IS GENERATED?
WHAT COSTS SCALE WITH ACTIVITY?
WHAT COSTS REMAIN FIXED?
```

Unit economics should not be invented before the activity is validated.

## 68. Public-Interest Economics

Not all value appears as direct commercial revenue.

Aurex's work may eventually create public-interest value through:

- improved reliability;
- reduced losses;
- better planning data;
- energy efficiency;
- resilience;
- interoperability;
- local technical capability;
- reduced dependency;
- better coordination of distributed energy resources.

Public-interest economics should still be evidence-based.

Aurex should avoid claiming public value that has not been measured, modeled or credibly justified.

## 75. Financial Decision Record

Material financial decisions should eventually be documented in a simple decision record.

A useful format is:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Decision | What was approved, rejected or deferred |
| Date | Traceability |
| Purpose | Why the decision matters |
| Amount or resource | Cash or in-kind value, if known |
| Funding source | Source and restrictions |
| Authority | Who had authority to decide |
| Conflicts | Disclosed interests or none known |
| Evidence | Basis for decision |
| Conditions | Reporting, milestones or obligations |
| Risk | Main financial or institutional risks |
| Record location | Where supporting documents are stored |

This supports traceability without requiring premature bureaucracy.

## 76. Minimum Financial Baseline — Now

At the current stage, Aurex should establish a minimum financial baseline:

1. Do not invent financial capacity that does not exist.
2. Record material founder-funded institutional expenses.
3. Separate personal and institutional finances when legal and operational structure permits.
4. Track material in-kind contributions.
5. Keep copies of material invoices, receipts, agreements and commitments.
6. Use mission, dependency, conflict and evidence tests before accepting material funding.
7. Avoid restricted funding unless restrictions can be tracked.
8. Do not accept obligations Aurex cannot responsibly deliver.
9. Treat founder/key-person financial concentration as a known limitation.
10. Add material financial risks to the institutional risk register.

This is the F0/F1 baseline.

## 77. Funding Decision Test

Before accepting material funding, Aurex should ask:

```text
MISSION - Does it advance the mission?
TRUTH - Does it preserve institutional truth?
EVIDENCE - What stage does it fund?
CONTROL - What authority does the funder receive?
RESTRICTION - How may funds be used?
DEPENDENCY - What reliance does it create?
DELIVERY - Can Aurex meet the obligations?
RISK - What risk enters the register?
RECORD - Can the decision be documented?
EXIT - What happens if the relationship ends?
```

If the answers are unclear, acceptance should wait.
