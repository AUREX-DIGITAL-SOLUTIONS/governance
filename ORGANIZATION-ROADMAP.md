---

## 1. Purpose

The Organization Roadmap establishes the sequence through which Aurex progresses from a founder-led institution under formation toward an organization capable of developing and stewarding credible digital energy infrastructure programmes.

The roadmap coordinates four parallel journeys:

```text
INSTITUTION
     +
TECHNOLOGY
     +
EVIDENCE
     +
ECOSYSTEM
     |
     v
INSTITUTIONAL MATURITY
```

None can safely advance indefinitely without the others.

---

## 2. Roadmap Principle

Traditional roadmaps often begin with dates.

Aurex should initially begin with **evidence gates**.

Instead of:

```text
2027 -> Facility EMS
2028 -> Regional DERMS
2029 -> National DERMS
```

Aurex should ask:

```text
WHAT MUST BE TRUE
      |
      v
WHAT EVIDENCE PROVES IT
      |
      v
WHAT CAPABILITY IS REQUIRED
      |
      v
WHO HAS AUTHORITY
      |
      v
WHAT RISK REMAINS
      |
      v
ARE WE READY TO ADVANCE?
```

Dates can be attached when dependencies, resources and commitments become sufficiently known.

## 14. Central Validation Question

Aurex's architecture currently assumes that meaningful visibility, interoperability and coordination gaps exist across distributed energy resources and facility-level energy infrastructure.

That assumption must be decomposed.

Research should determine:

```text
WHAT IS ALREADY VISIBLE?
WHAT IS NOT?
TO WHOM?
AT WHAT RESOLUTION?
THROUGH WHICH SYSTEM?
WHO OWNS THE DATA?
WHAT CANNOT INTEROPERATE?
WHAT OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE RESULTS?
WHO EXPERIENCES THE PROBLEM?
WHAT IS THE VALUE OF FIXING IT?
```

This is much stronger than merely stating:

> Kenya has a last-mile visibility gap.

The near-term research priority is to validate where meaningful visibility, interoperability and coordination gaps actually exist across Kenyan facility and distributed-energy infrastructure.

The distinction must remain explicit.

## 20. Stage 1 - Exit Gate

Proceed when there is credible evidence that:

1. a defined problem exists;
2. identified stakeholders experience it;
3. existing solutions do not fully address the selected use case;
4. Aurex has identified an addressable portion;
5. solving it could create meaningful value;
6. the proposed technical approach is plausible;
7. no obvious authority or regulatory contradiction makes the proposed experiment inappropriate.

If evidence rejects the original thesis:

> **Change the thesis.**

Do not manufacture validation.

## 28. Stage 3 - Controlled Pilot

Only after sufficient technical and problem validation should Aurex pursue a real-world pilot.

A pilot exists to answer:

> **Does the system create measurable value under real operating conditions?**

---

## 29. Pilot Selection

A pilot site should be selected because it provides useful evidence, not because it is simply available.

Selection criteria may include:

- representative energy assets;
- measurable problem;
- cooperative asset owner;
- legitimate authority;
- data availability;
- manageable safety risk;
- technical relevance;
- ability to establish baseline performance.

---

## 30. Pilot Baseline


## 31. Pilot Success Criteria

Success criteria should be defined **before** results exist.

Depending on the use case, they may include:

- improved visibility;
- reliable telemetry;
- higher renewable self-consumption;
- peak reduction;
- reduced generator runtime;
- improved fault detection;
- reduced energy cost;
- improved operational response.

Do not select metrics afterward merely because they improved.

## 37. Regional DERMS Is Not Yet National DERMS

A successful multi-facility demonstration establishes evidence for aggregation.

It does not establish:

- national authority;
- utility integration;
- regulatory approval;
- nationwide deployment;
- market participation rights.

## 43. Africa Scale

A system validated in Kenya cannot automatically be copied across Africa.

Countries differ in:

- regulation;
- utility structure;
- market design;
- infrastructure;
- languages;
- telecommunications;
- energy resources;
- institutions;
- economics.

Therefore:

> **Reuse globally. Validate locally.**

Kenyan success should create reusable capability, not assumptions about the rest of Africa.

## 59. Stop Gates

Aurex should stop or delay advancement when:

- the problem is not sufficiently evidenced;
- stakeholders do not experience the assumed need;
- the proposed intervention lacks legitimate authority;
- safety controls are insufficient;
- security risks are not understood;
- economics are implausible;
- capability is insufficient for the consequence;
- legal or regulatory contradictions are unresolved;
- claims are moving faster than evidence;
- the founder remains an unacceptable single point of failure for the proposed stage.

Stop gates are not failure. They protect institutional credibility.

---

## 60. Pivot Gates

Aurex should pivot when evidence shows that:

- the original problem framing is too broad;
- the strongest use case is different from the initial thesis;
- another stakeholder experiences the problem more directly;
- open-source components solve more of the problem than expected;
- the regulatory pathway requires a different role;
- facility-level value exists but regional aggregation does not yet;
- technical capability is useful but operational authority belongs elsewhere.

A pivot should be documented as institutional learning, not hidden as inconsistency.

## 71. Roadmap Doctrine

Aurex's roadmap doctrine is:

- evidence gates before date-driven commitments;
- institution before uncontrolled scale;
- problem before solution;
- evidence before investment;
- prototype before pilot;
- pilot before programme;
- authority before operational control;
- capability before scale;
- safety before optimization;
- coordinate globally, control safely locally;
- reuse globally, validate locally.

Technical capability does not create legal, regulatory or operational authority.

National DERMS and the Energy Operating System remain programme and architectural vision until progressively validated.

National-scale work requires legitimate institutional collaboration.
