# Task 18 - Research & Innovation Framework
## Aurex Digital Solutions

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Foundational Draft / Draft v1.0  
**Institution:** Aurex Digital Solutions  
**Phase:** External / Institutional Development  
**Scope:** Research governance, evidence generation, experimentation, innovation, technology validation and institutional learning  
**Navigation:** [Previous: Partnership & Stakeholder Framework](./PARTNERSHIP-AND-STAKEHOLDER-FRAMEWORK.md) | [Home: Documentation Index](./README.md) | [Next: Programme Governance Framework](./PROGRAMME-GOVERNANCE-FRAMEWORK.md)

Task 17 established how Aurex learns from and collaborates with its external ecosystem.

Task 18 establishes how Aurex converts **questions, uncertainty and real-world energy problems into reliable knowledge and validated technology.**

> **Research exists to reduce uncertainty—not to manufacture evidence for conclusions Aurex has already decided to believe.**

---

## 1. Purpose

Aurex operates in a field where technical ambition can easily outrun evidence.

The Research & Innovation Framework establishes how Aurex should:

- identify research problems;
- formulate questions and hypotheses;
- gather evidence;
- conduct experiments;
- validate technologies;
- document findings;
- distinguish evidence from assumptions;
- manage research risk;
- collaborate with researchers;
- convert findings into engineering requirements;
- decide whether innovations should advance, change or stop.

Research provides the bridge between Aurex's **vision** and what can actually be demonstrated.

---

## 2. Research Doctrine

Aurex adopts:

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
EVIDENCE
   ↓
UNDERSTANDING
   ↓
HYPOTHESIS
   ↓
EXPERIMENT
   ↓
RESULT
   ↓
DECISION
```

Not:

```text
DESIRED CONCLUSION
        ↓
SEARCH FOR SUPPORTING EVIDENCE
```

Research must be allowed to reject, modify, pivot or stop Aurex programmes, concepts and hypotheses.

---

## 3. Research Principles

Aurex research should follow:

1. **Problem before technology**
2. **Evidence before conclusion**
3. **Hypothesis before claim**
4. **Method before result**
5. **Reproducibility where practical**
6. **Negative results remain results**
7. **Uncertainty should be visible**
8. **Claims should match evidence**
9. **Research independence**
10. **Local validation before local claims**
11. **Safety before experimentation**
12. **Research should inform decisions**

---

## 4. Research Is Not Advocacy

Aurex may strongly believe in a technical direction.

Research still must be able to conclude:

```text
THE PROBLEM IS DIFFERENT
THE PROBLEM IS SMALLER
THE SOLUTION IS WRONG
THE TECHNOLOGY IS INADEQUATE
THE ECONOMICS DO NOT WORK
THE ASSUMPTION WAS FALSE
THE PROGRAMME SHOULD STOP
```

Institutional credibility depends on this freedom.

---

Aurex's research system should connect:

## 7. Research Agenda

Aurex should maintain a research agenda rather than pursue unrelated technical questions indefinitely.

The agenda should answer:

```text
WHAT DO WE NEED TO KNOW
TO MAKE THE NEXT IMPORTANT DECISION?
```

This connects research directly to Task 15's evidence gates.

---

## 8. Research Portfolio

Research may be classified as:

### Foundational Research

Understanding systems, technologies and concepts.

### Applied Research

Investigating specific energy problems.

### Experimental Research

Testing technical hypotheses.

### Programme Research

Producing evidence required for programme decisions.

### Operational Research

Learning from deployed or pilot systems.

Not every research activity must immediately create a product.

---

## 9. Problem Discovery

Research should begin with the problem.

Potential sources include:

- operational observations;
- literature;
- stakeholder interviews;
- public datasets;
- engineering failures;
- policy developments;
- technology changes;
- programme needs;
- field evidence.

A technology looking for a problem should not automatically become an institutional research priority.

---

## 10. Problem Statement

A research problem should describe:

```text
CURRENT CONDITION
        ↓
OBSERVED / SUSPECTED GAP
        ↓
WHO OR WHAT IS AFFECTED
        ↓
WHY IT MATTERS
        ↓
WHAT IS UNKNOWN
```

The unknown creates the research need.

---

## 11. Research Questions

Good research questions should be:

- specific enough to investigate;
- relevant to a decision;
- answerable with available or obtainable evidence;
- neutral enough to permit unexpected answers.

Example:

> **To what extent do heterogeneous distributed energy systems at Kenyan facilities lack interoperable operational visibility, and what measurable operational consequences result?**

This is stronger than:

> **Why does Kenya need Aurex's Facility EMS?**

---

## 12. Hypotheses

A hypothesis is a testable proposition.

Example:

> **Integrating heterogeneous facility energy resources through a common Facility EMS can materially improve operational visibility compared with the facility's existing fragmented monitoring approach.**

A hypothesis is not a fact.

---

## 13. Hypothesis Register

Material Aurex hypotheses may eventually be recorded with:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis ID | Traceability |
| Statement | Testable proposition |
| Origin | Why it exists |
| Evidence required | What would test it |
| Method | How it will be tested |
| Status | Proposed/testing/supported/rejected |
| Findings | Result |
| Decision impact | What changes |

This prevents assumptions from silently becoming institutional facts.

---

## 14. Assumption Management

Not every assumption requires a formal research project.

But material assumptions should follow:

```text
ASSUMPTION
    ↓
WHY IT MATTERS
    ↓
EVIDENCE REQUIRED
    ↓
VALIDATE
    ↓
CONFIRM / MODIFY / REJECT
```

Task 13 should track assumptions whose failure creates material risk.

---

## 15. Evidence Classification

Aurex should consistently distinguish:

### Verified Fact

Supported by reliable evidence.

### Observation

Something directly observed in a defined context.

### Reasonable Inference

A conclusion logically derived from available evidence.

### Hypothesis

A proposition requiring testing.

### Assumption

Something temporarily treated as true for planning or analysis.

### Estimate

A calculated or judged approximation.

### Simulation Result

Evidence generated from a model.

### Experimental Result

Evidence generated under controlled conditions.

### Vision

A desired future state.

These categories must not be silently exchanged.

---

## 16. Evidence Hierarchy

Evidence quality depends on the question.

Potential evidence sources include:

```text
PRIMARY OPERATIONAL DATA
CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT
OFFICIAL / PRIMARY DOCUMENT
PEER-REVIEWED RESEARCH
TECHNICAL STANDARD
REPUTABLE INSTITUTIONAL RESEARCH
STAKEHOLDER EVIDENCE
SECONDARY ANALYSIS
EXPERT OPINION
ANECDOTE
```

This is not a universal rigid ranking.

A regulator's official rule may be the strongest evidence for a regulatory question, while measured telemetry may be stronger for equipment performance.

---

## 17. Source Quality

Research should evaluate:

- authority;
- methodology;
- date;
- relevance;
- conflicts;
- geographic context;
- sample;
- reproducibility;
- consistency with other evidence.

A widely repeated statistic is not necessarily a reliable statistic.

---

## 18. Source Freshness

Energy systems evolve.

Research should distinguish:

```text
CURRENT SYSTEM STATE
        ≠
HISTORICAL SYSTEM STATE
```

Sources should be checked for:

- publication date;
- data period;
- superseding regulation;
- updated infrastructure;
- changed market conditions.

---

## 19. Literature Review

Before significant research or engineering, Aurex should understand what is already known.

Literature review may include:

- academic papers;
- standards;
- government reports;
- regulator publications;
- utility publications;
- technical documentation;
- open-source documentation;
- industry research.

Research should avoid rediscovering solved problems unnecessarily.

---

## 20. Primary Sources

Where practical, Aurex should prioritize primary sources for material factual claims.

For Kenya energy research, this may include appropriate publications from:

- government institutions;
- regulators;
- utilities;
- system institutions;
- development institutions;
- technology maintainers.

The specific source should match the claim.

---

## 21. Research Method

Before collecting evidence, define:

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
HYPOTHESIS
   ↓
METHOD
   ↓
DATA REQUIRED
   ↓
ANALYSIS METHOD
   ↓
SUCCESS / REJECTION CRITERIA
```

This reduces the temptation to redefine success after seeing results.

---

## 22. Methodology Proportionality

Not every question requires academic-scale methodology.

Aurex may use:

- desk research;
- interviews;
- surveys;
- data analysis;
- simulation;
- laboratory experiments;
- prototype tests;
- hardware-in-the-loop;
- field studies.

Rigor should scale with consequence.

---

## 23. Research Records

Material research should retain enough information to understand:

- question;
- method;
- sources;
- assumptions;
- data;
- tools;
- results;
- limitations;
- conclusions;
- decisions.

The goal is institutional memory and reproducibility.

---

## 24. Research Reproducibility

Where practical, another competent person should be able to understand how Aurex reached a material research result.

For technical research this may include:

- source data;
- configuration;
- software version;
- experiment procedure;
- hardware;
- analysis scripts;
- environment;
- assumptions.

Reproducibility requirements should consider security, confidentiality and data rights.

---

## 25. Data Collection

Aurex should collect data because a research question requires it.

Not because:

> "We may need it someday."

Research data collection should follow:

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
MINIMUM DATA REQUIRED
   ↓
AUTHORIZED COLLECTION
   ↓
SECURE STORAGE
   ↓
ANALYSIS
   ↓
RETENTION / DISPOSAL
```

---

## 26. Data Quality

Research should consider:

- completeness;
- accuracy;
- timestamp quality;
- calibration;
- sampling rate;
- missing data;
- measurement error;
- bias;
- context.

More data does not automatically mean better evidence.

---

## 27. Data Provenance

Aurex should know:

```text
WHERE DID THE DATA COME FROM?
WHO COLLECTED IT?
HOW?
WHEN?
WITH WHAT DEVICE / METHOD?
HAS IT BEEN MODIFIED?
```

This is particularly important for AI and operational energy research.

---

## 28. Sensitive Research Data

Research may involve:

- facility telemetry;
- infrastructure details;
- energy consumption;
- commercial information;
- personal information;
- security-sensitive information.

Research openness does not override Task 11 or Task 12.

---

## 29. Research Ethics

Research should respect:

- legitimate rights;
- privacy;
- safety;
- informed participation where applicable;
- confidentiality;
- data rights;
- public interest;
- applicable law.

Research value does not justify unlimited collection or experimentation.

---

## 30. Human Participants

If Aurex conducts research involving people, the level of ethical governance should match the activity and applicable requirements.

Interviews, surveys and behavioral studies may require consideration of:

- informed participation;
- privacy;
- data use;
- vulnerability;
- potential harm.

Formal ethics review should be introduced where legally or institutionally required.

---

## 31. Experimental Safety

Experiments involving physical energy systems require additional controls.

```text
EXPERIMENTAL VALUE
       ≠
AUTHORITY TO CREATE UNCONTROLLED PHYSICAL RISK
```

Task 11 and Task 13 apply.

---

## 32. Experimental Progression

High-consequence technical experiments should progress approximately through:

```text
SIMULATION
    ↓
SOFTWARE TEST
    ↓
LAB
    ↓
HARDWARE-IN-THE-LOOP
    ↓
CONTROLLED PHYSICAL TEST
    ↓
AUTHORIZED FIELD VALIDATION
```

Not every technology requires every stage.

---

## 33. Simulation

Simulation allows Aurex to test:

- control strategies;
- energy flows;
- failure scenarios;
- optimization;
- scaling assumptions.

But:

> **Simulation evidence is evidence about the model—not automatic proof about the physical world.**

---

## 34. Laboratory Research

Lab environments allow controlled testing of:

- communications;
- devices;
- protocols;
- controllers;
- failure behavior;
- cybersecurity;
- interoperability.

Lab results should document environmental limitations.

---

## 35. Hardware-in-the-Loop

Hardware-in-the-loop may help test control systems against realistic simulated energy-system behavior before higher-risk physical deployment.

It can provide stronger evidence than pure software simulation while maintaining controlled conditions.

---

## 36. Field Research

Field research introduces:

- real equipment;
- real communications;
- human operators;
- environmental variation;
- operational constraints.

Therefore field evidence can reveal problems invisible in laboratory conditions.

It also introduces higher risk.

---

## 37. Field Authority

Before field experimentation:

```text
SITE AUTHORITY?
ASSET AUTHORITY?
DATA AUTHORITY?
CONTROL AUTHORITY?
SAFETY RESPONSIBILITY?
REGULATORY REQUIREMENTS?
```

must be understood.

Research status does not create authority.

---

## 38. Facility EMS Research Programme

Aurex's near-term technical research should focus on the smallest useful operational layer:

> **The facility.**

The central question is not yet whether Aurex can coordinate a country.

It is whether heterogeneous energy resources at a real facility can be observed and coordinated in a useful, interoperable and safe way.

---

## 39. Facility EMS Research Questions

Examples include:

- Which resources commonly exist?
- Which protocols expose them?
- What telemetry is available?
- What data is missing?
- How fragmented is monitoring?
- What systems already exist?
- Can heterogeneous resources be normalized?
- What coordination creates value?
- What should remain locally controlled?
- What are installation costs?
- What are cybersecurity risks?
- What measurable outcome improves?

---

## 40. Last-Mile Visibility Research

Aurex should treat **last-mile visibility gap** as a research framing until validated.

The research question should be:

> **Where, specifically, do meaningful operational visibility and coordination gaps remain across Kenya's increasingly distributed energy infrastructure, and what portion can a Facility EMS credibly address?**

This prevents Aurex's language from becoming a claimed national finding before evidence exists.

---

## 41. Existing Systems First

Before claiming a gap, research should identify existing capabilities such as:

- smart meters;
- SCADA;
- GIS;
- distribution automation;
- facility BMS/EMS;
- inverter portals;
- vendor monitoring;
- AMI;
- utility systems;
- DER platforms.

Aurex should identify the **remaining problem**, not pretend existing infrastructure does not exist.

---

## 42. Gap Analysis

A useful structure:

```text
REQUIRED CAPABILITY
        ↓
EXISTING CAPABILITY
        ↓
LIMITATION
        ↓
OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE
        ↓
ADDRESSABLE GAP
```

This produces a much stronger problem statement.

---

## 43. Technology Scouting

Aurex should systematically evaluate existing technologies before building new ones.

Questions include:

- Does it already solve the requirement?
- Is it open?
- Is it interoperable?
- Is it maintained?
- Is it secure?
- Is it scalable?
- Can Aurex contribute upstream?
- What dependency does adoption create?

---

## 44. Build vs Reuse

Aurex should ask:

```text
REUSE?
EXTEND?
INTEGRATE?
CONTRIBUTE UPSTREAM?
BUILD?
```

Building from zero should be a deliberate decision, not an engineering reflex.

---

## 45. Open-Source Research

Open-source technologies can accelerate experimentation because architecture and implementation may be inspectable.

But open source still requires evaluation for:

- maturity;
- security;
- maintainability;
- license;
- community;
- architecture;
- compatibility.

Popularity is not sufficient validation.

---

## 46. Innovation Definition

Innovation should not mean:

> using fashionable technology.

For Aurex:

> **Innovation is the development or application of knowledge, technology, architecture or operating approaches that create materially improved outcomes for a validated problem.**

---

## 47. Innovation Pipeline

Aurex should use:

```text
PROBLEM
   ↓
INSIGHT
   ↓
HYPOTHESIS
   ↓
CONCEPT
   ↓
EXPERIMENT
   ↓
PROTOTYPE
   ↓
VALIDATION
   ↓
PILOT
   ↓
PROGRAMME
   ↓
SCALE
```

Not every concept should survive the pipeline.

---

## 48. Innovation Portfolio

Innovation activity may include:

### Incremental

Improve existing systems.

### Integrative

Connect previously fragmented systems.

### Architectural

Change how components interact.

### Transformational

Enable fundamentally new system behavior.

Aurex's interoperability mission may often create value through **integration and architecture**, not invention of every component.

---

## 49. Technology Readiness

Technology maturity should be explicitly represented.

Aurex may use a simple internal model:

```text
R0 — IDEA
R1 — RESEARCH
R2 — EXPERIMENT
R3 — PROTOTYPE
R4 — LAB VALIDATED
R5 — FIELD VALIDATED
R6 — PILOT
R7 — OPERATIONAL
R8 — SCALE-READY
```

This is an internal maturity model, not a claim of alignment with any external TRL standard unless formally mapped.

---

## 50. Maturity Is Evidence-Based

Moving from one readiness level to another requires evidence.

```text
WE BUILT IT
      ≠
IT WORKS

IT WORKED ONCE
      ≠
IT IS RELIABLE

IT WORKED IN LAB
      ≠
IT WORKS IN FIELD

IT WORKED IN PILOT
      ≠
IT IS SCALE-READY
```

---

## 51. Research-to-Engineering Handoff

Research should produce engineering inputs.

```text
RESEARCH FINDING
       ↓
VALIDATED REQUIREMENT
       ↓
ARCHITECTURE DECISION
       ↓
ENGINEERING WORK
```

A research conclusion should not silently become code without defining the requirement it creates.

---

## 52. Engineering-to-Research Feedback

The relationship is bidirectional.

Engineering may discover:

- unexpected limitations;
- missing data;
- new failure modes;
- protocol problems;
- user needs.

These should generate new research questions.

```text
RESEARCH ↔ ENGINEERING
```

---

## 53. Research-to-Programme Handoff

Before programme investment:

```text
PROBLEM EVIDENCE
      +
STAKEHOLDER EVIDENCE
      +
TECHNICAL EVIDENCE
      +
ECONOMIC EVIDENCE
      +
RISK UNDERSTANDING
      ↓
PROGRAMME DECISION
```

Task 19 governs the programme itself.

---

## 54. Pilot Is Research and Delivery

An early pilot often serves two purposes:

1. demonstrate value;
2. generate evidence.

Therefore pilot design should include explicit learning objectives.

A pilot that only attempts to "look successful" wastes a major research opportunity.

---

## 55. Success Criteria

Before experiments or pilots, define success where practical.

Examples:

- telemetry reliability;
- device compatibility;
- response time;
- data completeness;
- peak reduction;
- renewable utilization;
- generator runtime;
- fault detection;
- installation time.

Criteria should relate to the hypothesis.

---

## 56. Failure Criteria

Aurex should also define evidence that would weaken or reject a hypothesis.

Otherwise every result can be interpreted as success.

This is essential for institutional learning.

---

## 57. Negative Results

A failed experiment is not wasted if it answers the question honestly.

Negative results should be recorded because they can:

- prevent repeated failure;
- expose false assumptions;
- redirect engineering;
- narrow the problem;
- improve safety;
- protect resources;
- strengthen credibility.

Aurex should not hide negative results merely because they weaken a preferred story.

---

## 58. Research Stop Gates

Research should be able to stop a line of work.

Stop conditions may include:

- the problem is not validated;
- the proposed solution does not address the problem;
- safety risk is unacceptable;
- economics are implausible;
- required authority cannot be obtained;
- existing systems already solve the need;
- evidence no longer supports continuation.

Stopping weak work preserves resources for stronger work.

---

## 59. Research Pivot Gates

Research may also justify a pivot rather than a full stop.

A pivot should document:

```text
ORIGINAL HYPOTHESIS
        ↓
NEW EVIDENCE
        ↓
WHAT CHANGED
        ↓
REVISED HYPOTHESIS
        ↓
DECISION
```

Pivots should be evidence-led, not branding adjustments after failure.

---

## 60. Peer Review

Material research should be challenged before it supports major decisions.

Peer review may include:

- internal technical review;
- independent expert review;
- partner review where conflicts are managed;
- open-source community review;
- academic or institutional review where appropriate.

Review should test method, evidence, assumptions and conclusions.

---

## 61. Independent Validation

Higher-consequence claims require stronger validation.

Independent validation becomes more important when research is used for:

- safety-related control;
- government engagement;
- funding claims;
- public-interest justification;
- field deployment;
- national-scale programme decisions.

Aurex should not claim independent validation before it exists.

---

## 62. Research Conflicts

Research can be influenced by incentives.

Potential conflicts include:

- funding source preferences;
- partner commercial interests;
- founder attachment to an idea;
- supplier influence;
- publication incentives;
- advocacy pressure;
- desire to win approval or funding.

Conflicts do not automatically invalidate research, but they must be visible and managed.

---

## 63. Funding and Research Independence

Funding may support research.

Funding must not purchase conclusions.

Research agreements should preserve:

- methodological integrity;
- right to document limitations;
- ability to report negative results appropriately;
- data rights;
- IP clarity;
- publication and confidentiality boundaries;
- decision independence.

This connects directly to Task 16 and Task 17.

---

## 64. Research Partnerships

Research partnerships may eventually involve universities, utilities, public institutions, private facilities, technology companies, open-source communities or development institutions.

A relationship should not be represented as a research partnership until the role, authority, scope and evidence contribution are defined.

No current researchers, committees, ethics boards, field sites, pilots, datasets, approvals or independent validations are created by this framework.

---

## 65. Publication and Open Research

Aurex may eventually publish research, methods, datasets, software or findings where appropriate.

Publication decisions should consider:

- public interest;
- security;
- privacy;
- commercial confidentiality;
- data rights;
- partner obligations;
- reproducibility;
- institutional credibility.

Open research should not expose sensitive infrastructure information or unauthorized data.

---

## 66. Research Claims

Aurex communications should use verbs that match evidence.

Examples:

| Evidence state | Appropriate verbs |
|---|---|
| Direct observation | observed |
| Measurement | measured |
| Model output | simulated |
| Approximation | estimate |
| Evidence-based reasoning | infer |
| Testable proposition | hypothesize |
| Future concept | propose |
| Sufficient tested evidence | validated |

Aurex should not silently promote assumptions, simulations, aspirations or proposed concepts into facts.

---

## 67. Statistical and Analytical Integrity

Quantitative research should protect against false precision.

Where relevant, analysis should consider:

- sample size;
- sampling bias;
- measurement uncertainty;
- baseline definition;
- control or comparison condition;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- outliers;
- missing data;
- model assumptions;
- sensitivity analysis.

A precise number is not automatically an accurate conclusion.

---

## 68. Uncertainty

Uncertainty should be visible in research outputs.

Research records should distinguish:

```text
WHAT WE KNOW
WHAT WE OBSERVED
WHAT WE INFER
WHAT WE HYPOTHESIZE
WHAT WE ASSUME
WHAT WE DO NOT YET KNOW
```

Visible uncertainty improves decisions because it prevents premature certainty.

---

## 69. AI-Assisted Research

AI tools may support research by helping with:

- literature discovery;
- summarization;
- coding;
- data cleaning;
- simulation support;
- drafting;
- hypothesis exploration;
- documentation.

But:

```text
AI OUTPUT
    ≠
VERIFIED EVIDENCE
```

AI may accelerate research work, but it does not remove the obligation to verify sources, methods, data and conclusions.

---

## 70. AI Validation

AI-assisted research should be validated according to consequence.

For material research claims, Aurex should check:

- source existence;
- source relevance;
- quote accuracy;
- data provenance;
- calculation correctness;
- methodological fit;
- hidden assumptions;
- hallucinated references;
- overconfident synthesis.

The more consequential the claim, the less acceptable unsupported AI synthesis becomes.

---

## 71. Citation Integrity

Research citations should support the exact claim being made.

A source about one country, year, technology or regulatory condition should not be generalized beyond its evidence.

Citation integrity requires distinguishing:

```text
SOURCE SAYS
      ≠
AUREX INFERS
      ≠
AUREX PROPOSES
```

---

## 72. AI Analysis

AI-generated analysis should not be treated as independent validation.

Where AI supports analysis, records should identify:

- tool or model used where relevant;
- prompt or method summary where appropriate;
- source data;
- human review;
- verification steps;
- limitations.

AI can be useful inside a research process, but it should not become the authority for a research conclusion.

---

## 73. Local Validation

Aurex should be careful when importing evidence from other contexts.

A technology or operating model that works elsewhere may still require local validation for:

- grid conditions;
- facility types;
- regulation;
- procurement norms;
- communications reliability;
- technical skills;
- maintenance capacity;
- economics;
- user behavior;
- institutional authority.

Local validation does not mean ignoring global evidence. It means testing whether that evidence transfers.

---

## 74. African Research Capability

Aurex should contribute to African technical research capability rather than depend entirely on imported conclusions.

This may eventually include:

- local datasets;
- local field studies;
- open technical documentation;
- African researcher participation;
- university collaboration;
- shared testing methods;
- public-interest technology evaluation;
- locally relevant standards participation.

This is an ambition, not a present staffing or partnership claim.

---

## 75. Research Knowledge Base

Aurex should progressively maintain a research knowledge base for material work.

It may include:

- research questions;
- hypotheses;
- literature notes;
- source records;
- datasets;
- experiments;
- findings;
- decision records;
- rejected assumptions;
- open questions.

Research should not disappear into informal chat or personal memory.

---

## 76. Research IDs

Material research items should eventually receive stable identifiers.

Examples:

```text
RQ-001  Research Question
HYP-001 Hypothesis
EXP-001 Experiment
DS-001  Dataset
FND-001 Finding
DR-001  Decision Record
```

Identifiers support traceability from question to evidence to decision.

---

## 77. Research Status

A simple research status model may include:

```text
PROPOSED
SCOPED
ACTIVE
PAUSED
UNDER REVIEW
SUPPORTED
REJECTED
PIVOTED
ARCHIVED
```

Status should reflect evidence and decision state, not enthusiasm.

---

## 78. Research Decision Records

Material research decisions should be recorded when they affect engineering, programmes, funding, partnerships or public claims.

A record may include:

- question;
- evidence reviewed;
- options;
- uncertainty;
- decision;
- authority;
- consequence;
- next action.

This connects research to institutional accountability.

---

## 79. Research Performance

Research performance should not be measured only by activity.

Useful measures may include:

- key uncertainties reduced;
- hypotheses tested;
- assumptions rejected or confirmed;
- evidence gates passed;
- engineering requirements clarified;
- risks identified early;
- decisions improved;
- negative results documented;
- reusable knowledge created.

Research that prevents a bad programme can be high-value research.

---

## 80. Innovation Performance

Innovation performance should eventually consider whether innovation creates validated value.

Possible measures include:

- validated problem fit;
- improvement over existing systems;
- reliability;
- safety;
- interoperability;
- maintainability;
- user usefulness;
- cost-effectiveness;
- scalability evidence;
- public-interest contribution.

Novelty alone is not sufficient.

## 81. Research Risk

Research creates risk as well as knowledge.

Risks may include:

- safety risk;
- cybersecurity risk;
- privacy risk;
- misleading claims;
- poor methodology;
- partner dependency;
- IP exposure;
- misuse of data;
- overinvestment in weak hypotheses;
- premature public commitments.

Research risk should feed Task 13.

## 82. Research Security

Research involving energy infrastructure can expose sensitive operational information.

Security controls should address:

- access to datasets;
- storage of telemetry;
- disclosure of vulnerabilities;
- field-site information;
- equipment configurations;
- credentials and secrets;
- export or sharing of sensitive material.

Research openness must be balanced with responsible technology obligations.

## 83. Intellectual Property

Research may generate intellectual property, open-source contributions, datasets, methods, designs or trade secrets.

Aurex should clarify:

- ownership;
- licensing;
- contributor rights;
- partner rights;
- publication rights;
- open-source compatibility;
- confidentiality;
- downstream use.

Tasks 9, 10, 16 and 17 apply.

## 84. Research Governance

At the current stage, Aurex's research governance may be lightweight and founder-led.

As consequence grows, governance should progressively introduce:

- research review;
- method approval for material work;
- risk review;
- ethics review where required;
- security review;
- independent challenge;
- documented decision authority.

Governance should scale with consequence, not bureaucracy for its own sake.

## 85. Current Founder-Led Research

Aurex's current research capacity is founder-led.

This means the founder may presently perform several functions:

- identifying questions;
- reviewing literature;
- forming hypotheses;
- developing technical concepts;
- documenting frameworks;
- assessing early evidence;
- making founding-stage decisions.

This concentration should be documented honestly.

It is a formation-stage reality, not evidence of a full research department, independent review board, active university consortium or validated research programme.

Independent challenge should be introduced progressively for higher-consequence research.


This describes research-system maturity, not technology readiness.

## 88. Near-Term Facility EMS Research

Near-term Facility EMS research should establish whether a facility-level system can credibly address a validated problem.

It should avoid claiming national impact before evidence exists.

Questions should include:

```text
WHAT FACILITY TYPES?
WHAT EXISTING SYSTEMS?
WHAT RESOURCES?
WHAT DATA?
WHAT CONTROL AUTHORITY?
WHAT OPERATIONAL PAIN?
WHAT MEASURABLE IMPROVEMENT?
WHAT RISKS?
WHAT COST?
WHAT NEXT EVIDENCE GATE?
```

---

## 89. Facility EMS Evidence Gate

A Facility EMS should not progress merely because it has been built.

The evidence gate should ask:

- Is there a validated facility-level problem?
- What existing systems already address it?
- What remaining gap exists?
- Can Aurex integrate heterogeneous resources safely?
- Is telemetry reliable enough?
- Does coordination improve a measurable outcome?
- Are cybersecurity and safety risks controlled?
- Can operation be maintained?
- What evidence supports the next maturity level?

A working demonstration is not the same as validated usefulness.

---

## 90. Regional DERMS Research Gate

A regional DERMS concept should not follow automatically from a Facility EMS.

The research gate should require:

- multiple facility evidence;
- distribution-level stakeholder evidence;
- utility or system-operational relevance;
- regulatory and authority analysis;
- communications and interoperability evidence;
- aggregation logic;
- safety and cybersecurity assurance;
- economic justification;
- institutional willingness.

Facility evidence may inform regional research, but it does not prove regional need.

---

## 91. National DERMS Research Gate

A national DERMS concept requires a much higher evidence threshold.

Aurex should progress through:

```text
FACILITY EVIDENCE
      ↓
MULTI-FACILITY EVIDENCE
      ↓
REGIONAL EVIDENCE
      ↓
INSTITUTIONAL EVIDENCE
      ↓
NATIONAL-SCALE HYPOTHESIS
      ↓
NATIONAL PROGRAMME RESEARCH
```

A working Facility EMS must not be represented as evidence that a National DERMS should automatically exist.

---

## 92. National Research Doctrine

National-scale claims require national-scale evidence or carefully limited language.

Aurex should not claim:

```text
KENYA HAS NO DIGITAL SYSTEMS
KENYA HAS PROVEN A NATIONAL LAST-MILE VISIBILITY GAP
AUREX HAS VALIDATED A NATIONAL DERMS NEED
```

unless appropriate evidence and authority exist.

Until then, Aurex may state research questions, hypotheses and proposed investigation paths.

The Energy Operating System remains a long-term research and architectural vision.

It should be treated as:

- a future architecture concept;
- a programme hypothesis;
- an integration vision;
- a research direction;
- a possible institutional contribution.

It should not be represented as a deployed platform, authorized national system or validated national operating layer.

At the current stage, Aurex should maintain at least:

- a hypothesis register for major technical claims;
- basic research records;

## 95. Research Decision Test

Before research supports a material decision, ask:

```text
WHAT QUESTION WAS ASKED?
WHAT METHOD WAS USED?
WHAT EVIDENCE EXISTS?
WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SHOW?
WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAIN?
WHAT ASSUMPTIONS REMAIN?
WHAT CLAIM CAN LEGITIMATELY BE MADE?
WHAT DECISION SHOULD CHANGE?
```

If these cannot be answered, the research is not ready to support a high-consequence decision.

## 96. Research Integrity Equation

Aurex research integrity depends on:

```text
CLEAR QUESTION
+
APPROPRIATE METHOD
+
RELIABLE EVIDENCE
+
VISIBLE UNCERTAINTY
+
HONEST CLAIMS
+
DOCUMENTED DECISIONS
```

Weakness in one part should be visible, not hidden.

## 97. Innovation Equation

Aurex innovation should satisfy:

```text
VALIDATED PROBLEM
+
USEFUL INSIGHT
+
APPROPRIATE TECHNOLOGY
+
EVIDENCE OF IMPROVEMENT
+
SAFE IMPLEMENTATION
+
RESPONSIBLE GOVERNANCE
```

Without a validated problem and evidence of improvement, technology remains activity rather than innovation.
