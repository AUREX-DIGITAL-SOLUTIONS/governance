# Task 5 - Code of Conduct

## Aurex Digital Solutions

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Complete / Draft v1.0  
**Institution:** Aurex Digital Solutions  
**Scope:** Founders, leadership, staff, contributors, contractors, researchers, advisors, partners, community participants, and anyone representing Aurex Digital Solutions

> This Code of Conduct defines expected behavior for participation in Aurex Digital Solutions. It does not claim that Aurex Digital Solutions is currently a government agency, licensed utility, regulator, national grid operator, deployed critical-infrastructure operator, or government-authorized energy-system operator.

Task 4 defined what Aurex values and the institutional principles that should guide decisions.

Task 5 translates those values and principles into expectations for human behavior.

```text
Values describe what Aurex believes.
Principles guide institutional decisions.
The Code of Conduct defines how people are expected to behave.
```

## 1. Purpose

Aurex Digital Solutions is being built to research, develop, validate, and steward open digital energy coordination infrastructure for African energy systems, beginning from Kenya.

This work may involve software, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, energy data, open-source communities, research, public-interest partnerships, institutional engagement, and safety-relevant technical systems.

The Code exists to ensure that participation in Aurex remains:

**professional, respectful, safe, transparent, technically rigorous, collaborative, evidence-driven, and accountable.**

Aurex's behavioral standard must match the seriousness of the domains in which it intends to work. Energy infrastructure, public-interest software, research, and institutional partnerships require discipline before authority, evidence before claims, and safety before speed.

## 2. Applicability

This Code applies wherever someone participates in Aurex-related work or represents Aurex Digital Solutions, including:

- repositories and technical collaboration spaces;
- meetings, workshops, and working sessions;
- research activities and publications;
- laboratories, demonstrations, tests, pilots, and field visits;
- partner, academic, institutional, and community engagements;
- email, messaging, calls, forums, and online communities;
- conferences, public events, and consultations;
- procurement, contracting, and advisory work;
- social media or public communication when speaking on behalf of Aurex.

The Code applies regardless of seniority, role, title, contribution level, nationality, employment status, or institutional affiliation.

## 3. Core Standard

Everyone participating in Aurex work is expected to:

- act with honesty, professionalism, and care;
- respect people, institutions, communities, and lawful authority;
- protect safety, privacy, security, and public trust;
- distinguish evidence from opinion, ambition, assumption, and speculation;
- acknowledge uncertainty and limitations;
- avoid exaggerating institutional status, technical maturity, authorization, or deployment readiness;
- work collaboratively and constructively;
- give proper credit for ideas, work, data, code, and contributions;
- disclose conflicts of interest;
- raise concerns when something may be unsafe, misleading, unethical, unlawful, or harmful.

The core standard is simple:

> Do the work seriously, treat people fairly, protect public trust, and never make claims that the evidence, authority, or maturity of the institution does not support.

## 4. Respect and Professional Conduct

Aurex participants must communicate and collaborate respectfully.

Respectful conduct includes:

- listening in good faith;
- making room for technical disagreement without personal hostility;
- challenging ideas with evidence and reasoning;
- avoiding humiliation, intimidation, exclusion, and retaliation;
- using professional language in institutional settings;
- honoring time commitments or communicating changes promptly;
- treating junior participants, community members, and external partners with the same dignity given to senior stakeholders;
- recognizing that public-interest infrastructure work requires trust as well as competence.

Professional disagreement is allowed and expected. Personal attacks, coercive behavior, discriminatory conduct, and abuse of authority are not.

## 5. Prohibited Conduct

The following conduct is prohibited in Aurex spaces and work:

- harassment, bullying, threats, intimidation, or abusive communication;
- discrimination based on legally or ethically protected characteristics, including race, ethnicity, nationality, gender, religion, disability, age, sexual orientation, or socioeconomic background;
- sexual harassment or unwelcome sexual attention;
- retaliation against anyone who raises a concern or participates in a review process;
- deliberate misrepresentation of facts, authority, evidence, role, employment, affiliation, maturity, or deployment status;
- unauthorized access to systems, data, accounts, devices, facilities, or infrastructure;
- misuse of confidential, private, proprietary, security-sensitive, or personal information;
- plagiarism, unattributed copying, or false credit claims;
- bribery, improper inducement, or undisclosed influence;
- reckless publication of sensitive security, operational, personal, or partner information;
- behavior that undermines safety, legal compliance, public trust, or institutional integrity.

This list is not exhaustive. Conduct may violate the Code when it is inconsistent with the institution's public-interest, safety-first, evidence-driven, and integrity-centered obligations.

## 6. Technical Integrity

Aurex participants must treat technical work as a matter of institutional responsibility.

Technical integrity requires:

- accurate documentation of assumptions, methods, dependencies, constraints, and results;
- testing claims before presenting them as proven;
- distinguishing prototypes, demonstrations, pilots, production systems, and operational infrastructure;
- avoiding safety, reliability, cybersecurity, or interoperability claims that have not been validated;
- documenting known limitations and failure modes;
- reviewing work proportionately to its risk;
- refusing shortcuts that create unacceptable safety, security, legal, or public-trust risks.

No participant should present experimental software, research models, simulations, or prototypes as deployed critical infrastructure.

## 7. Evidence and Intellectual Honesty

Aurex is an evidence-driven institution.

Participants must:

- cite sources where claims depend on external evidence;
- separate measured facts from interpretation;
- avoid cherry-picking evidence;
- disclose uncertainty, incomplete data, and methodological limitations;
- correct errors when discovered;
- avoid overstating impact, readiness, adoption, regulatory support, or stakeholder endorsement;
- maintain records sufficient for review and institutional learning.

Ambition is acceptable. Unsupported certainty is not.

## 8. Safety Responsibility

Energy, software, automation, cybersecurity, and artificial intelligence can affect people, facilities, institutions, and public trust.

Every Aurex participant has a duty to consider safety before action.

This includes:

- avoiding unauthorized control or interference with physical systems;
- escalating hazards, vulnerabilities, and unsafe assumptions;
- designing tests so they do not endanger people, facilities, devices, operations, or data;
- respecting operational procedures at partner or host facilities;
- refusing to bypass safety controls for speed, convenience, or demonstration value;
- documenting risks that may affect users, operators, institutions, or communities.

Aurex should build toward safety maturity before seeking operational authority.

## 9. Authority and Permission

Aurex participants must not act beyond documented authority.

No person may:

- access systems, facilities, accounts, data, devices, networks, or infrastructure without permission;
- imply authorization from a government, regulator, utility, system operator, funder, partner, or community unless that authorization exists and is documented;
- present Aurex as a public authority, licensed utility, regulator, national grid operator, or deployed critical-infrastructure institution unless that status is legally true;
- commit Aurex to obligations without appropriate institutional approval;
- use another institution's name, data, logo, systems, or endorsement without permission.

Authority must be explicit, proportionate, recorded, and reviewable.

## 10. Cybersecurity Conduct

Cybersecurity conduct must be disciplined, lawful, and permission-based.

Participants must:

- use only authorized accounts, credentials, devices, tools, and environments;
- protect credentials, tokens, keys, and access permissions;
- report suspected vulnerabilities through approved channels;
- avoid publishing exploit details, sensitive configurations, or partner vulnerabilities without authorization;
- avoid scanning, testing, probing, or accessing external systems without documented permission;
- follow responsible disclosure practices;
- treat energy-related digital systems as potentially safety-relevant even when they appear small or experimental.

Security work is not an excuse to bypass consent, law, partner trust, or safety procedure.

## 11. Data Responsibility

Aurex participants must handle data carefully.

Data responsibility includes:

- collecting only data that is necessary and authorized;
- documenting source, permission, quality, scope, and limitations;
- protecting personal, confidential, proprietary, commercially sensitive, security-sensitive, and operational data;
- using appropriate access controls;
- avoiding unauthorized transfer, publication, reuse, or model training;
- respecting data rights and contractual obligations;
- correcting or removing data that was collected or used improperly.

Energy data can reveal behavior, operations, vulnerabilities, economic conditions, and institutional patterns. It must not be treated casually.

## 12. Privacy

Aurex participants must respect privacy in research, software, operations, communications, and community engagement.

Participants should:

- minimize collection of personal information;
- seek informed consent where required;
- explain how information will be used;
- protect private communications and personal records;
- avoid exposing individuals through datasets, screenshots, logs, demos, or publications;
- anonymize or aggregate information where appropriate;
- comply with applicable privacy laws and institutional obligations.

Privacy is part of public-interest infrastructure, not an afterthought.

## 13. Open-Source Conduct

Aurex may build and steward open-source tools. Open-source work must remain professional, secure, inclusive, and accountable.

Expected conduct includes:

- respectful issue, pull request, and review participation;
- clear contribution rules;
- transparent licensing and permission boundaries;
- maintainable documentation;
- security-aware disclosure and release practices;
- constructive technical criticism;
- proper attribution and acknowledgment;
- protection against hostile, abusive, or bad-faith participation.

Open-source availability does not mean operational readiness, safety certification, public authority, or permission to deploy in safety-critical contexts.

## 14. Attribution and Credit

Aurex participants must give fair credit.

This applies to:

- code;
- documentation;
- research;
- datasets;
- designs;
- analysis;
- field insights;
- community knowledge;
- partner contributions;
- advisory input;
- prior art and open-source work.

Credit should be accurate, visible where appropriate, and consistent with licenses, agreements, and ethical practice.

No participant may claim another person's work as their own or erase material contributions from the institutional record.

## 15. AI Use

Aurex may use artificial intelligence for research, software development, analysis, documentation, operations, and decision support.

AI use must be responsible.

Participants must:

- review AI-assisted work before relying on it;
- verify factual, technical, legal, financial, and safety-relevant claims;
- disclose AI assistance where required by policy, publication standards, partner agreements, or law;
- avoid entering confidential, personal, proprietary, security-sensitive, or partner-restricted information into tools that are not approved for that use;
- avoid presenting AI-generated analysis as measured evidence;
- document important assumptions, limitations, and human review;
- ensure that AI does not replace accountable human judgment in safety-relevant or institutionally binding decisions.

AI can support Aurex's work. It must not become a substitute for responsibility.

## 16. Conflicts of Interest

Participants must disclose actual, potential, or perceived conflicts of interest.

Conflicts may arise from:

- employment, consulting, advisory, or board roles;
- ownership interests or investments;
- family or close personal relationships;
- procurement, vendor, or partnership decisions;
- research funding;
- political, regulatory, or institutional affiliations;
- gifts, travel, hospitality, or favors.

Disclosure does not automatically prohibit participation. Concealment is the institutional risk.

When a conflict exists, Aurex should document it and decide whether recusal, review, disclosure, limits on access, or other controls are necessary.

## 17. Gifts, Bribery, and Improper Influence

Aurex participants must not offer, solicit, accept, or facilitate bribes, kickbacks, secret commissions, improper inducements, or personal benefits intended to influence decisions.

Gifts, hospitality, travel, sponsorships, or favors must not compromise judgment or create the appearance of improper influence.

Participants must disclose anything that could reasonably be viewed as influencing:

- procurement;
- hiring;
- research outcomes;
- technical evaluation;
- partnership selection;
- public communication;
- funding decisions;
- access to data, systems, or institutional relationships.

Public-interest work requires independence from improper influence.

## 18. Representation of Aurex

Participants must represent Aurex accurately.

No person may speak on behalf of Aurex unless authorized to do so.

When representing Aurex, participants must:

- state the institution's status truthfully;
- avoid overstating maturity, deployment, authorization, endorsement, funding, partnerships, or technical readiness;
- distinguish personal opinions from institutional positions;
- avoid making commitments beyond their authority;
- respect confidentiality and partner communication requirements;
- protect the institution's public-interest, safety-first, and evidence-driven posture.

Aurex should be described as an institution being built unless and until its legal, operational, and authorization status changes.

## 19. Government and Public-Institution Engagement

Aurex may engage government bodies, regulators, utilities, counties, public agencies, universities, and development institutions.

Such engagement must be respectful, transparent, lawful, and accurate.

Participants must:

- avoid implying public authorization before it exists;
- avoid presenting programme concepts as official public programmes unless formally authorized;
- respect procurement, ethics, consultation, and conflict-of-interest rules;
- document meetings, commitments, and follow-up responsibilities appropriately;
- protect sensitive public or institutional information;
- separate advocacy, research, partnership exploration, and formal authorization.

Public-institution engagement must strengthen trust, not borrow authority prematurely.

## 20. Community Conduct

Aurex's work may affect or involve communities, users, facilities, developers, students, researchers, and public-interest groups.

Participants must:

- communicate plainly and honestly;
- avoid extractive engagement;
- respect local context and lived experience;
- avoid promising benefits before validation, funding, authorization, or delivery capacity exists;
- treat community feedback as evidence for learning;
- protect participants from exposure, harm, or misuse of their information;
- avoid using communities as symbolic proof without genuine engagement and consent.

Community trust must be earned through conduct, not claimed through branding.

## 21. Inclusion and Fair Participation

Aurex should create conditions for fair participation.

This includes:

- welcoming qualified and good-faith contributors from different backgrounds;
- reducing unnecessary barriers to participation;
- making expectations clear;
- recognizing different forms of contribution;
- ensuring meetings, reviews, and decisions are not dominated by authority alone;
- addressing discriminatory or exclusionary behavior;
- supporting learning while maintaining standards.

Inclusion does not lower the bar for evidence, safety, or technical quality. It widens the path for capable people to help meet that bar.

## 22. Research Conduct

Aurex research must be honest, careful, and reviewable.

Participants must:

- define research questions clearly;
- document methods, assumptions, datasets, tools, and limitations;
- avoid fabricating, falsifying, or selectively presenting results;
- respect human-subject, privacy, consent, and institutional-review requirements where applicable;
- disclose funding, conflicts, and material constraints;
- protect sensitive partner, facility, operational, and personal information;
- avoid overstating early findings as general proof.

Research should serve institutional learning and public-interest outcomes.

## 23. Partner Conduct

Partners and partner representatives working with Aurex are expected to follow this Code within Aurex-related activities.

Aurex participants must also treat partners with professionalism.

Partner conduct includes:

- honoring agreements and documented permissions;
- respecting partner systems, staff, facilities, data, brand, and operating procedures;
- avoiding unauthorized public claims about partner endorsement or implementation;
- documenting responsibilities and decision rights;
- escalating safety, security, legal, or reputational concerns promptly;
- refusing improper pressure from any partner or stakeholder.

Partnership should not compromise safety, independence, evidence, or public-interest purpose.

## 24. Reporting Concerns

Participants should report concerns when they believe conduct may violate this Code or create safety, security, legal, ethical, privacy, data, research, or public-trust risk.

Concerns may include:

- harassment, discrimination, bullying, or retaliation;
- unauthorized access or unsafe technical behavior;
- misleading claims;
- data misuse or privacy risk;
- research misconduct;
- conflicts of interest;
- bribery or improper influence;
- partner, community, or public-institution concerns;
- conduct that may damage safety, integrity, or public trust.

Until formal reporting channels are established, concerns should be raised to the appropriate Aurex founder, leadership contact, project maintainer, or designated institutional representative.

As the organization matures, Aurex should establish documented reporting channels, escalation paths, confidentiality expectations, investigation procedures, and recordkeeping practices.

## 25. Non-Retaliation

Aurex prohibits retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, asks a compliance question, refuses unsafe or unauthorized work, participates in a review process, or helps correct institutional risk.

Retaliation may include:

- dismissal, exclusion, demotion, or loss of opportunity;
- threats, intimidation, or harassment;
- hostile treatment;
- reputational attacks;
- pressure to withdraw a concern;
- punitive changes to access, role, credit, or participation.

Bad-faith false reports may themselves violate the Code. Honest reports that are mistaken must not be punished.

## 26. Enforcement

Violations of this Code may result in proportionate action.

Depending on the severity, context, and role involved, actions may include:

- clarification of expectations;
- warning;
- required correction or apology;
- removal of content;
- restriction of access;
- recusal from decisions;
- removal from a project, repository, meeting, or community space;
- termination of a contract or contribution relationship;
- referral to appropriate institutional, legal, security, or public authorities where required.

Enforcement should protect people, safety, data, systems, institutional integrity, and public trust.

## 27. Fair Process

Code enforcement must be fair, documented, and proportionate.

Fair process includes:

- taking concerns seriously;
- avoiding conflicts of interest in review;
- giving affected parties a reasonable opportunity to provide relevant information where appropriate;
- protecting confidentiality to the extent possible;
- documenting decisions and reasons;
- applying standards consistently;
- distinguishing error, negligence, misconduct, and deliberate harm;
- correcting institutional failures as well as individual behavior.

Fair process does not require Aurex to tolerate ongoing unsafe, abusive, unlawful, or high-risk conduct while review is pending.

## 28. Leadership Responsibility

Leaders, founders, maintainers, managers, advisors, and anyone with authority have heightened responsibilities.

They must:

- model the Code in their own behavior;
- avoid using authority to silence concerns or bypass review;
- set clear expectations;
- protect fair participation;
- respond to safety, security, ethical, legal, and conduct concerns;
- disclose conflicts;
- avoid overstating institutional status or maturity;
- preserve evidence and decision records;
- build the systems needed for accountability as Aurex matures.

Leadership is a duty of stewardship, not exemption.

## 29. Speaking-Up Duty

Participants have a duty to speak up when silence would allow serious risk to continue.

This duty is especially important where there may be:

- safety risk;
- cybersecurity risk;
- data or privacy risk;
- unlawful or unauthorized conduct;
- research misconduct;
- misleading public claims;
- improper influence;
- harm to communities, users, partners, or public institutions;
- abuse of authority.

Speaking up should be done responsibly, with relevant evidence and through appropriate channels whenever possible.

## 30. Expected Contributor Behavior

Contributors are expected to:

- read and follow relevant documentation;
- ask questions when expectations, permissions, or safety boundaries are unclear;
- communicate respectfully;
- make focused, reviewable contributions;
- document changes and assumptions;
- respect maintainers' decisions and review processes;
- report bugs, vulnerabilities, or risks responsibly;
- avoid introducing code, data, licenses, or dependencies they do not have the right to contribute;
- accept correction when work does not meet the required standard.

Good contribution is not only about output. It is about reliability, honesty, respect, and stewardship.

## 31. Code of Conduct Pledge

Participants in Aurex Digital Solutions pledge to:

```text
Act with integrity.
Respect people and institutions.
Protect safety, privacy, security, and public trust.
Use evidence honestly.
Work within authority and permission.
Give fair credit.
Disclose conflicts.
Challenge unsafe or misleading claims.
Build open digital energy infrastructure responsibly.
```
