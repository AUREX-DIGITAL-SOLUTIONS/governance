# Task 5 - Code of Conduct

## Aurex Digital Solutions

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Complete / Draft v1.0  
**Institution:** Aurex Digital Solutions  
**Scope:** Everyone participating in or representing Aurex
**Document type:** Code of conduct

> This Code defines expected behavior. It does not grant operational, regulatory, utility, or system-operator authority.

## 1. Purpose and Applicability

Aurex works with open digital energy infrastructure, research, data, software, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, public-interest partnerships, and potentially safety-relevant systems. Participation must therefore be professional, respectful, safe, transparent, evidence-driven, collaborative, and accountable.

This Code applies to founders, leaders, staff, contractors, researchers, contributors, advisors, partners, community participants, and anyone representing Aurex. It applies in repositories, meetings, research, pilots, field work, partner engagements, procurement, public communication, and online spaces.

Leadership and seniority do not create an exemption.

The behavioral standard follows the [Values & Institutional Principles](docs/01-institutional-foundation/VALUES-AND-INSTITUTIONAL-PRINCIPLES.md). Specialized requirements are defined in the [Contributing Framework](docs/02-technical-governance/CONTRIBUTING-FRAMEWORK.md), [Security & Responsible Technology Policy](docs/02-technical-governance/SECURITY-AND-RESPONSIBLE-TECHNOLOGY-POLICY.md), and [Ethics, Sovereignty & Public-Interest Framework](docs/02-technical-governance/ETHICS-SOVEREIGNTY-AND-PUBLIC-INTEREST-FRAMEWORK.md).

## 2. Core Standard

Participants must:

- act honestly, respectfully, professionally, and with care;
- respect people, communities, institutions, cultures, and lawful authority;
- protect safety, privacy, security, data, and public trust;
- distinguish evidence from opinion, assumption, ambition, and speculation;
- acknowledge uncertainty, limitations, risks, and errors;
- represent Aurex's role, status, maturity, authorization, and partnerships accurately;
- give proper credit for ideas, work, data, code, and contributions;
- disclose conflicts of interest and improper influence;
- raise concerns about unsafe, misleading, unethical, unlawful, or harmful conduct.

> Do the work seriously, treat people fairly, protect public trust, and never claim more than the evidence, authority, or maturity supports.

## 3. Respectful Participation

Aurex expects good-faith collaboration. Participants should listen, communicate clearly, challenge ideas with evidence, welcome technical disagreement, honor commitments, and treat junior participants, communities, partners, and external stakeholders with equal dignity.

The following are unacceptable:

- harassment, bullying, threats, intimidation, abuse, or retaliation;
- discrimination or sexual harassment;
- personal attacks, humiliation, coercion, or exclusion;
- misuse of authority or hostile behavior toward someone who raises a concern;
- conduct that undermines safety, legal compliance, institutional integrity, or public trust.

Aurex should reduce unnecessary barriers to participation while maintaining standards for evidence, safety, and technical quality.

## 4. Integrity and Representation

Participants must not:

- deliberately misrepresent facts, evidence, roles, affiliation, authority, maturity, deployment, funding, endorsement, or results;
- present experimental software, simulations, prototypes, or research as production or critical infrastructure;
- fabricate, falsify, plagiarize, selectively manipulate, or conceal material evidence;
- claim another person's work or erase material contributions;
- make commitments on behalf of Aurex without appropriate authority;
- present Aurex as a government agency, licensed utility, regulator, grid operator, deployed national platform, or government-approved programme unless that status is legally true.

Important technical and institutional claims should be documented, reviewable, and corrected when errors are found. The [Research & Innovation Framework](docs/03-operations-and-development/RESEARCH-AND-INNOVATION-FRAMEWORK.md) and [Governance Framework](docs/01-institutional-foundation/GOVERNANCE-FRAMEWORK.md) define supporting evidence and decision practices.

## 5. Safety, Permission, and Security

Participants must work within documented authority and permission. No one may access or control systems, facilities, accounts, devices, networks, data, or infrastructure without authorization.

Participants must:

- put human safety, equipment protection, reliability, cybersecurity, and critical-service continuity before speed or optimization;
- design tests to avoid harm to people, facilities, operations, devices, and data;
- follow host and partner safety procedures;
- protect credentials, keys, access permissions, and sensitive configurations;
- report hazards and vulnerabilities through approved channels;
- never scan, probe, test, or access external systems without documented permission;
- avoid publishing sensitive security, operational, personal, or partner information without authorization.

The [Security & Responsible Technology Policy](docs/02-technical-governance/SECURITY-AND-RESPONSIBLE-TECHNOLOGY-POLICY.md), [Risk Management Framework](docs/03-operations-and-development/RISK-MANAGEMENT-FRAMEWORK.md), and [Legal & Institutional Readiness](docs/04-readiness-and-baseline/LEGAL-AND-INSTITUTIONAL-READINESS.md) provide the detailed controls.

## 6. Data, Privacy, and AI

Participants must collect and use data only for defined, authorized purposes. They must document provenance and limitations, apply appropriate access and retention controls, protect personal, confidential, proprietary, commercial, operational, and security-sensitive information, and respect consent, licenses, contracts, and applicable law.

AI-assisted work must be reviewed by a responsible person. Participants must verify factual, technical, legal, financial, and safety-relevant outputs; avoid entering restricted information into unapproved tools; distinguish generated analysis from measured evidence; and keep accountable human judgment in safety-relevant or institutionally binding decisions.

Detailed data, privacy, and responsible-AI requirements belong in the [Security & Responsible Technology Policy](docs/02-technical-governance/SECURITY-AND-RESPONSIBLE-TECHNOLOGY-POLICY.md) and [Ethics, Sovereignty & Public-Interest Framework](docs/02-technical-governance/ETHICS-SOVEREIGNTY-AND-PUBLIC-INTEREST-FRAMEWORK.md).

## 7. Open-Source and Research Conduct

Open-source and research participation must be respectful, maintainable, secure, properly attributed, and consistent with applicable licenses and permissions. Contributors must submit work they have the right to submit, document assumptions and limitations, report vulnerabilities responsibly, and avoid presenting early findings as general proof.

The [Open-Source & Licensing Framework](docs/02-technical-governance/OPEN-SOURCE-AND-LICENSING-FRAMEWORK.md), [Contributing Framework](docs/02-technical-governance/CONTRIBUTING-FRAMEWORK.md), and [Research & Innovation Framework](docs/03-operations-and-development/RESEARCH-AND-INNOVATION-FRAMEWORK.md) define the relevant contribution, licensing, publication, and research practices.

## 8. Conflicts and Improper Influence

Participants must disclose actual, potential, or perceived conflicts of interest, including employment, consulting, ownership, family relationships, procurement, funding, political or regulatory affiliations, gifts, travel, hospitality, or other benefits.

Disclosure does not automatically prohibit participation. Aurex may require recusal, review, access limits, public disclosure, or other controls. Bribery, kickbacks, secret commissions, improper inducements, and benefits intended to influence decisions are prohibited.

## 9. Partners, Communities, and Public Institutions

Participants must honor agreements, permissions, confidentiality, operating procedures, data rights, and partner or community safety. They must communicate plainly, avoid extractive engagement, respect local context and lived experience, and never promise benefits, endorsement, authorization, or delivery before the evidence and capacity exist.

Engagement with governments, regulators, utilities, counties, universities, communities, funders, and development institutions must be lawful, transparent, and accurate. Aurex must not borrow public authority through language, branding, or implied endorsement.

Partnership and public-interest expectations are developed in the [Partnership & Stakeholder Framework](docs/03-operations-and-development/PARTNERSHIP-AND-STAKEHOLDER-FRAMEWORK.md), [Ethics, Sovereignty & Public-Interest Framework](docs/02-technical-governance/ETHICS-SOVEREIGNTY-AND-PUBLIC-INTEREST-FRAMEWORK.md), and [Funding & Financial Framework](docs/03-operations-and-development/FUNDING-AND-FINANCIAL-FRAMEWORK.md).

## 10. Reporting and Non-Retaliation

Participants should report concerns involving harassment, discrimination, retaliation, unsafe or unauthorized work, security vulnerabilities, data misuse, misleading claims, research misconduct, conflicts, bribery, or harm to people, communities, partners, systems, or public trust.

Until formal channels exist, concerns may be raised to the founder, appropriate leader, project maintainer, or designated institutional representative. As Aurex matures, it should establish documented reporting channels, escalation paths, confidentiality expectations, investigation procedures, and records.

Aurex prohibits retaliation against anyone who raises a good-faith concern, asks a compliance question, refuses unsafe or unauthorized work, participates in review, or helps correct risk. A mistaken honest report is not bad faith.

## 11. Enforcement and Fair Process

Violations may result in proportionate action, including clarification, correction, warning, removal of content, access restriction, recusal, removal from a project or community, termination of a relationship, or referral to appropriate authorities.

Reviews should take concerns seriously, manage conflicts of interest, protect confidentiality where possible, give affected parties a reasonable opportunity to provide relevant information, document decisions and reasons, apply standards consistently, and distinguish error, negligence, misconduct, and deliberate harm.

Aurex may take immediate protective action where ongoing conduct creates serious safety, security, legal, abusive, or public-trust risk.

## 12. Leadership Responsibility

Founders, leaders, maintainers, managers, advisors, and anyone with authority must model this Code, set clear expectations, protect participation, disclose conflicts, respond to concerns, preserve records, and avoid using authority to silence review or bypass safety, legal, or governance requirements.

Leadership is a duty of stewardship, not an exemption.

## 13. Contributor Pledge

Participants in Aurex pledge to:

```text
Act with integrity.
Respect people and institutions.
Protect safety, privacy, security, and public trust.
Use evidence honestly.
Work within authority and permission.
Give fair credit.
Disclose conflicts.
Challenge unsafe or misleading claims.
Build open digital energy infrastructure responsibly.
```
