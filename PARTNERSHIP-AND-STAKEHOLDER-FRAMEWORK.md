# Task 17 - Partnership & Stakeholder Framework
## Aurex Digital Solutions

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Foundational Draft / Draft v1.0  
**Institution:** Aurex Digital Solutions  
**Phase:** External / Institutional Development  
**Scope:** Stakeholder identification, engagement, collaboration, partnership governance and institutional relationship management  
**Navigation:** [Previous: Funding & Financial Framework](./FUNDING-AND-FINANCIAL-FRAMEWORK.md) | [Home: Documentation Index](./README.md) | [Next: Research & Innovation Framework](./RESEARCH-AND-INNOVATION-FRAMEWORK.md)

Task 16 established how Aurex governs **capital and financial relationships**.

Task 17 establishes how Aurex governs **relationships with the institutions and people required to pursue its mission**.

> **Aurex should collaborate broadly without surrendering institutional truth, legitimate authority, technical integrity or public-interest responsibility.**

This framework defines relationship discipline without inventing current partners, government relationships, utility relationships, endorsements, memoranda of understanding, contracts, approvals, pilots, stakeholder meetings, funders, investors, universities, contributors or programme participants.

Categories and examples in this document are future or possible relationship classes unless separately evidenced in Aurex records.

---

## 1. Purpose

Aurex cannot build digital energy infrastructure in isolation.

Its work may eventually intersect with:

- energy users;
- facility owners;
- utilities;
- regulators;
- government institutions;
- technology providers;
- open-source communities;
- universities;
- researchers;
- development institutions;
- investors and funders;
- engineering companies;
- communities;
- infrastructure operators.

The Partnership & Stakeholder Framework defines how these relationships are identified, evaluated, established, governed, documented and, where necessary, ended.

```text
   ≠
PARTNERSHIP
   ≠
```

Public communication must preserve those distinctions.

## 3. Why Stakeholder Governance Matters

Digital energy infrastructure exists inside an institutional ecosystem.

A technically functional system can still fail because:

- users do not need it;
- operators cannot maintain it;
- utilities cannot integrate it;
- regulators cannot permit required activity;
- asset owners reject it;
- incentives conflict;
- partners withdraw;
- data rights are unclear;
- responsibilities are undefined.

Therefore:

> **Stakeholder alignment is part of infrastructure engineering.**

---

## 4. Stakeholder Before Partnership

Not every stakeholder should become a partner.

A stakeholder is broadly:

> A person, community, organization or institution that can affect, is affected by, or has a legitimate interest in Aurex's activities or their outcomes.

A partnership is a more deliberate relationship involving agreed cooperation.

Aurex should not treat stakeholder identification as a partner pipeline by default. Some stakeholders should only be consulted, informed, studied, monitored, protected, or respected.

## 7. Stakeholder Mapping

Before material engagement, Aurex should understand:

```text
WHO ARE THEY?
      |
      v
WHAT IS THEIR ROLE?
      |
      v
WHAT AUTHORITY DO THEY HAVE?
      |
      v
WHAT INTEREST DO THEY HAVE?
      |
      v
HOW COULD AUREX AFFECT THEM?
      |
      v
HOW COULD THEY AFFECT AUREX?
      |
      v
WHAT RELATIONSHIP IS APPROPRIATE?
```

This prevents approaching every stakeholder with the same message.

---

## 8. Authority Mapping

Energy-sector stakeholders have different authority.

Aurex should explicitly distinguish:

```text
POLICY AUTHORITY
REGULATORY AUTHORITY
ASSET OWNERSHIP
SYSTEM-OPERATION AUTHORITY
COMMERCIAL AUTHORITY
TECHNICAL EXPERTISE
FUNDING CAPACITY
COMMUNITY INTEREST
```

One does not automatically imply another.

A technology company, for example, may possess strong expertise without regulatory authority.

A funder may possess funding capacity without technical, regulatory or operational authority.

A government official may participate in discussion without creating procurement authority, regulatory approval or national endorsement.

---

## 9. Interest-Influence Mapping

Stakeholders may be mapped according to:

- level of legitimate interest;
- ability to affect Aurex's objectives;
- degree to which Aurex's activities may affect them.

This helps determine engagement intensity.

It should not be used to dismiss stakeholders with limited institutional power where Aurex's work materially affects them.

---

## 10. Engagement Principles

Aurex stakeholder engagement should be:

- truthful;
- purposeful;
- respectful;
- evidence-based;
- appropriately documented;
- proportionate;
- non-coercive;
- aware of authority boundaries;
- sensitive to public interest;
- open to disagreement.

Engagement should advance understanding before it advances narrative.

---

## 11. Engagement Begins With Learning

At Aurex's current stage, many external engagements should begin as **discovery**.

The initial question should usually be:

> **What problem actually exists from your perspective?**

rather than:

> **How can we deploy our solution?**

This protects Aurex from confirmation bias.

Engagement at Aurex's current stage should primarily learn and validate before selling or pitching predetermined national solutions.

---

## 12. Stakeholder Discovery

Discovery conversations can investigate:

- current workflows;
- energy infrastructure;
- pain points;
- existing digital systems;
- visibility;
- interoperability;
- maintenance;
- reliability;
- data availability;
- operational constraints;
- regulatory constraints;
- economic incentives.

The objective is evidence.

Aurex should not convert stakeholder observations into national facts without evidence.

```text
LONG-TERM VISION
```

Government-ready communication should keep these categories separate.

## 15. Relationship Levels L0-L5

A useful model is:

### L0 - Awareness

Aurex knows the stakeholder exists.

### L1 - Contact

Initial communication has occurred.

### L2 - Engagement

Meaningful discussion or information exchange has occurred.

### L3 - Collaboration

Specific work is undertaken together.

### L4 - Formal Partnership

Responsibilities are formally agreed.

### L5 - Strategic Relationship

A long-term material institutional relationship exists.

Progression is not automatic.

Aurex should not publicly describe a relationship above the level supported by evidence.

## 16. Meeting Doctrine

A meeting means only that a meeting occurred.

Therefore:

```text
MEETING
   ≠
ENDORSEMENT
   ≠
PARTNERSHIP
   ≠
CONTRACT
   ≠
APPROVAL
   ≠
AUTHORIZATION
```

This applies especially to government, utilities, regulators, universities, donors and major companies.

## 17. Government Engagement

Government engagement may eventually involve:

- research;
- policy discussions;
- technical consultation;
- innovation programmes;
- public infrastructure;
- programme development;
- procurement.

Aurex should clearly identify:

- which institution;
- its statutory role;
- the officials involved;
- purpose of engagement;
- what was actually discussed;
- whether anything was formally agreed.

---

## 18. Government Representation

Aurex must not convert:

> "We presented the concept to a government official."

into:

> "The government supports Aurex."

without evidence of that support.

Similarly:

```text
DISCUSSION ≠ APPROVAL
LETTER ≠ CONTRACT
MOU ≠ PROCUREMENT
PILOT DISCUSSION ≠ PILOT AUTHORIZATION
```

Public claims should match documentary evidence.

---

## 19. Regulatory Engagement

Regulators occupy a special position.

A regulator may:

- interpret requirements;
- enforce regulations;
- issue approvals or licenses where legally empowered;
- establish technical or market rules.

Engagement with a regulator does not make Aurex regulated, licensed, approved or endorsed unless the relevant formal process actually establishes that status.

Aurex should never treat regulatory conversation as permission to operate outside applicable law.

---

## 20. Utility Engagement

Utilities may be:

- potential users;
- system operators;
- asset owners;
- data holders;
- technical collaborators;
- programme participants.

The exact role must be identified.

Aurex should not assume utilities need a proposed system merely because the architecture appears technically useful.

Utility discussion is not utility approval.

---

## 21. Asset Owners and Facility Operators

Facility-level work requires engagement with those responsible for the actual infrastructure.

They can provide critical evidence about:

- equipment;
- operations;
- energy cost;
- outages;
- maintenance;
- generator usage;
- solar and battery performance;
- control constraints.

Before operational interaction, authority must be explicit.

No Aurex research, prototype or tool should observe, connect to, or influence facility systems without appropriate permission from the relevant owner or operator.

---

## 22. Community Engagement

Energy infrastructure ultimately affects people.

Where Aurex's activities materially affect communities, engagement should consider:

- accessibility;
- affordability;
- privacy;
- local priorities;
- reliability;
- employment;
- disruption;
- safety;
- digital inclusion.

Community participation should be proportional to impact.

---

## 24. Universities and Research Institutions

- research;

## 25. Research Independence

A research partner should be able to disagree with Aurex.

A partnership designed only to confirm Aurex's thesis has limited scientific value.

> **Good research partnerships increase the probability of discovering the truth, not merely the probability of validating the founder's idea.**

---

## 26. Open-Source Communities

Open-source communities are particularly important to Aurex's technology model.

Engagement should follow Task 9 and Task 10.

Aurex should:

- respect upstream governance;
- attribute work;
- contribute improvements where practical;
- avoid unnecessary forks;
- participate constructively;
- respect trademarks;
- respect licenses.

---

## 27. Upstream Relationship

Using an open-source project does not establish a partnership with its maintainers.

Likewise:

```text

    ≠
ENDORSEMENT

FORK
    ≠

```

Aurex should communicate these relationships accurately.

Use of an upstream open-source project is not a partnership or endorsement.

## 29. Vendor Neutrality

Aurex's interoperability mission means the institution should avoid unnecessary dependence on one vendor.

This does not prohibit strategic vendor relationships.

It requires that dependency be understood.

A vendor providing a component, tool, cloud service or integration pathway does not automatically become an institutional partner.

---

## 30. Engineering and Implementation Partners

External engineering organizations may provide capabilities Aurex does not yet possess internally.

Before relying on them, establish:

- scope;
- competence;
- safety responsibility;
- deliverables;
- authority;
- IP;
- confidentiality;
- liability;
- documentation;
- acceptance criteria.

Outsourcing work does not automatically outsource Aurex's accountability.

---

## 31. Development Institutions

Development institutions may support:

- research;
- energy access;
- infrastructure;
- innovation;
- capacity development;
- funding;
- technical assistance.

Aurex should understand both the opportunities and obligations associated with these relationships.

Development support should not be represented as institutional endorsement unless that is expressly and legitimately established.

---

## 32. Funders and Investors

Task 16 governs financial relationships.

Task 17 adds relationship governance.

A funder or investor may also become a strategic stakeholder.

However:

> **Capital contribution does not automatically create technical, regulatory or operational authority.**

Any governance rights must be explicit.

---

## 33. Partnership Definition

For Aurex, a partnership should mean:

> **A deliberately established relationship in which Aurex and one or more parties agree to cooperate toward defined objectives under understood responsibilities, authority and terms.**

This is stronger than informal collaboration.

Partnership claims require evidence of mutual agreement, not merely goodwill, discussion or aspiration.

---

## 34. Partnership Intake

Before creating a material partnership, answer:

```text
WHY THIS PARTNER?
WHAT OBJECTIVE?
WHAT DOES EACH PARTY CONTRIBUTE?
WHAT DOES EACH PARTY RECEIVE?
WHAT AUTHORITY IS REQUIRED?
WHAT RISKS EXIST?
WHAT DATA IS INVOLVED?
WHAT IP IS INVOLVED?
WHAT DEPENDENCY IS CREATED?
HOW DOES THE RELATIONSHIP END?
```

The intake record should be proportional to risk and consequence.

---

## 35. Strategic Fit

A partnership should be evaluated against:

- Aurex's mission;
- roadmap;
- values;
- technical strategy;
- public interest;
- financial sustainability;
- institutional independence.

A prestigious partner is not automatically a strategically useful partner.

---

## 36. Partner Due Diligence

Due diligence should scale with consequence.

Potential areas include:

- legal identity;
- reputation;
- technical capability;
- financial capacity;
- sanctions or legal concerns where applicable;
- security;
- ethics;
- conflicts;
- ownership;
- relevant track record.

A casual research conversation does not require the same diligence as critical-infrastructure collaboration.

---

## 37. Aurex Due Diligence

Partnership diligence should work both ways.

Aurex must accurately represent:

- its maturity;
- capabilities;
- resources;
- authority;
- limitations;
- legal status;
- programme status.

Aurex should not obtain partnerships through exaggerated claims.

---

For each partnership:

```text

        +
AUREX CONTRIBUTES
        |
        v

        |
        v
```

Contribution may include:

- equipment;
- data;

- software;

- personnel;
- facilities.

## 39. Partnership Authority

Only someone with appropriate Aurex authority should commit the institution.

Informal contributors should not create binding obligations on Aurex.

Likewise, not every employee of another organization can bind that organization.

Authority must be verified where commitments matter.

---

## 40. MOUs

A memorandum of understanding may be useful to establish mutual intent.

But:

```text
MOU
 ≠
PURCHASE ORDER
 ≠
DEPLOYMENT AUTHORITY
 ≠
REGULATORY APPROVAL
```

Its actual legal effect depends on its wording and applicable law.

Aurex should not infer more than the document establishes.

An MOU is not procurement authority, deployment authority or regulatory approval unless the document and lawful process actually create that authority.

---

## 41. Contracts

Where relationships involve material obligations, contracts may be appropriate.

Contracts should clearly address relevant matters such as:

- parties;
- scope;
- deliverables;
- responsibilities;
- payment;
- IP;
- confidentiality;
- data;
- liability;
- security;
- duration;
- termination;
- dispute handling.

Legal review should scale with consequence.

---

## 42. Data in Partnerships

Before sharing or receiving data, establish:

```text
WHAT DATA?
WHY?
WHO CONTROLS IT?
WHO MAY ACCESS IT?
HOW MAY IT BE USED?
HOW IS IT SECURED?
HOW LONG IS IT RETAINED?
MAY IT BE SHARED?
WHAT HAPPENS AT EXIT?
```

Open collaboration does not mean unrestricted data sharing.

---

## 43. Operational Energy Data

Operational energy data may reveal:

- facility behavior;
- occupancy patterns;
- industrial activity;
- infrastructure conditions;
- critical-system information.

Therefore:

> **Open-source software does not imply open operational telemetry.**

Operational telemetry should be governed according to authorization, security, privacy, commercial sensitivity and public-interest risk.

---

## 44. Intellectual Property

Partnerships should establish rights relating to:

- pre-existing IP;
- jointly developed IP;
- software;
- documentation;
- research;
- datasets;
- inventions;
- publications.

Agreements should remain compatible with Aurex's open-source strategy where relevant.

---

## 45. Background and Foreground IP

A useful distinction:

### Background IP

What each party already owned before collaboration.

### Foreground IP

What is created through collaboration.

Partnership terms should avoid accidentally transferring unrelated pre-existing rights.

---

## 46. Confidentiality

Some partnerships require confidential information.

Confidentiality should have:

- legitimate purpose;
- defined scope;
- appropriate protection;
- appropriate duration.

Confidentiality should not be used to conceal misconduct or fabricate public claims.

---

## 47. Partnership and Security

Partners may create access paths into:

- repositories;
- cloud systems;
- laboratories;

## 48. Partnership and Safety

No partner agreement should bypass legitimate safety controls.

A commercial deadline does not outrank physical safety.

> **Safety before optimization - and before partnership pressure.**

---

## 49. Partnership and Sovereignty

Task 12 applies directly.

Aurex should assess whether a partnership:

- builds African capability;
- preserves interoperability;
- preserves meaningful data control;
- enables knowledge transfer;
- avoids unnecessary lock-in;
- strengthens local institutions.

International collaboration is encouraged where it strengthens rather than permanently substitutes local capability.

---

## 50. Knowledge Transfer

For material technical partnerships, ask:

> **What capability remains after the partner leaves?**

Healthy collaboration should progressively leave:

- knowledge;
- documentation;
- skills;
- maintainable systems;
- institutional capability.

---

## 51. Dependency Risk

Aurex should identify dependencies on:

- individual partners;
- donors;
- technology providers;
- cloud platforms;
- upstream projects;
- equipment vendors;
- consultants.

Partnership concentration should be included in Task 13's Risk Register where material.

---

## 52. Conflict of Interest

Participants should disclose conflicts that could materially affect partnership decisions.

Examples include:

- financial interests;
- family relationships;
- competing roles;
- undisclosed commissions;
- supplier ownership.

Appropriate recusal should occur where required.

---

## 53. Anti-Bribery

Partnerships must comply with Aurex's anti-bribery principles.

Aurex should not provide improper benefits to obtain:

- contracts;
- approvals;
- regulatory treatment;
- funding;
- access;
- partnerships.

This applies to direct and indirect benefits.

---

## 54. Partner Representation

Partners should not be represented publicly beyond what is actually agreed.

Do not say:

> "Aurex is partnered with Organization X"

because representatives held discussions.

Partnership claims require a legitimate basis.

---

## 55. Logo Use

Using another institution's logo can imply endorsement.

Therefore partner logos should only be used where Aurex has appropriate permission and the use accurately reflects the relationship.

Likewise, Aurex should govern third-party use of its own identity.

Partner and logo claims require legitimate basis and permission.

---

## 56. Joint Communications

Material joint announcements should establish:

- what is being announced;
- relationship status;
- programme maturity;
- approved wording;
- spokespersons where appropriate.

Communications should not outrun the underlying agreement.

---

## 57. Stakeholder Records

Material engagements should be documented sufficiently to answer:

- who participated;
- when engagement occurred;
- the purpose of engagement;
- what was discussed;
- what was agreed;
- what was explicitly not agreed;
- what evidence was received;
- what follow-up is required;
- what relationship level applies.

Records should protect accuracy without creating unnecessary bureaucracy for low-risk contact.

---

## 58. Stakeholder Register

A stakeholder register should track material stakeholders and discovery targets.

Minimum fields may include:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder ID | Traceability |
| Name or category | Who or what is being tracked |
| Type | Facility, utility, regulator, vendor, community, researcher or other class |
| Authority type | Policy, regulatory, ownership, operational, commercial, technical, funding or community interest |
| Interest | Why the stakeholder matters |
| Influence | Ability to affect Aurex or be affected by Aurex |
| Relationship level | L0-L5 |
| Evidence basis | What supports the classification |
| Relationship owner | Aurex person accountable for continuity |
| Next action | Discovery, follow-up, review or close |
| Notes | Material context and limits |

The register should avoid inflated relationship labels.

---

## 59. Partnership Register

A partnership register should track formal and material collaborations.

Minimum fields may include:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Partnership ID | Traceability |
| Partner | Organization or party |
| Relationship level | L3-L5 where applicable |
| Objective | Why the relationship exists |
| Contribution model | What each party contributes |
| Authority basis | Who approved and under what authority |
| Agreement type | Informal collaboration, MOU, contract or other instrument |
| Data involved | Data categories and controls |
| IP involved | Background and foreground IP notes |
| Risk rating | Dependency, security, safety, finance, reputation and public-interest exposure |
| Start date | Beginning of material activity |
| Review date | Next governance review |
| Status | Proposed, active, paused, completed, exited or terminated |

The register should not list hoped-for partners as actual partners.

---

## 60. Relationship Ownership

Every material stakeholder or partnership relationship should have an Aurex owner.

The owner is responsible for:

- maintaining accurate records;
- coordinating communication;
- respecting authority boundaries;
- identifying risks;
- escalating commitments;
- ensuring follow-through;
- correcting inaccurate claims.

Relationship ownership is not authority to make commitments unless separately granted.

---

## 61. Partnership Performance and Review

Material partnerships should be reviewed periodically.

Review should ask:

1. What objective was the relationship meant to serve?
2. What has actually happened?
3. What evidence exists?
4. What value has each party contributed?
5. What risks have changed?
6. Are authority, data and IP terms still adequate?
7. Should the relationship continue, change or end?

A partnership that no longer serves the mission should be reconsidered even if it looks impressive externally.

---

## 62. Partnership Exit

Partnerships should be able to end without institutional collapse.

Exit planning should consider:

- unfinished obligations;
- data return or deletion;
- access revocation;
- IP rights;
- equipment or asset handover;
- communications;
- stakeholder notification;
- continuity of service where relevant;
- lessons learned.

Exit should be documented.

---

## 63. Immediate Termination

Immediate termination or suspension may be required where there is credible evidence of:

- corruption or bribery;
- serious security breach;
- serious safety risk;
- unlawful conduct;
- material misrepresentation;
- unauthorized public claims;
- misuse of data;
- abuse, harassment or coercion;
- unacceptable conflict of interest.

Termination decisions should follow applicable contracts, law and governance authority.

---

## 64. Disputes

Disputes may arise around scope, delivery, data, IP, payment, credit, public representation or authority.

Aurex should handle disputes through:

```text
CLARIFY FACTS
      |
      v
REVIEW AGREEMENTS
      |
      v
IDENTIFY AUTHORITY
      |
      v
SEEK RESOLUTION
      |
      v
ESCALATE WHERE REQUIRED
      |
      v
DOCUMENT OUTCOME
```

Disputes should not be hidden where they materially affect risk or public claims.

---

## 65. Stakeholder Feedback

Stakeholder feedback should be treated as evidence, not as command.

Aurex should examine:

- who provided the feedback;
- what authority or expertise they have;
- whether others report similar issues;
- what data supports the feedback;
- what tradeoffs the feedback implies;
- whether it changes mission, risk, roadmap or design assumptions.

Strong feedback should be incorporated into research and product learning loops.

---

## 66. Public-Interest Stakeholders

Some stakeholders may have little commercial influence but significant public-interest relevance.

These may include:

- affected communities;
- consumers;
- small businesses;
- local technicians;
- facility occupants;
- civil society organizations;
- public-interest researchers;
- vulnerable groups affected by reliability, affordability, privacy or safety risks.

Aurex should not define stakeholder importance only by purchasing power or institutional rank.

---

## 67. Partnership Selection and Rejection

Aurex should be willing to reject partnerships that:

- distort the mission;
- require inflated public claims;
- create unacceptable dependency;
- compromise public interest;
- weaken sovereignty;
- bypass safety or security controls;
- demand inappropriate exclusivity;
- conflict with open-source obligations;
- create unacceptable conflicts of interest;
- exceed Aurex's actual capacity.

Saying no can be an institutional control.

---

At the current founding stage, Aurex should prioritize learning, validation and evidence before formal partnership expansion.

The current-stage strategy is:

```text

      |
      v
VALIDATE USE CASES
      |
      v
BUILD TECHNICAL CAPABILITY
      |
      v
DEMONSTRATE RESPONSIBLE VALUE
      |
```

## 69. Near-Term Stakeholder Priority

For the Facility EMS and visibility research path, near-term discovery should focus on:

- facility operators who understand operational constraints;
- energy engineers who understand practical energy-system behavior;
- DER installers and integrators who understand device-level constraints;
- technology providers who understand interoperability and lifecycle issues;
- utilities that understand system-interface, data and operational concerns;
- regulatory and policy experts who understand legal and authorization boundaries;
- researchers who can challenge assumptions and strengthen methodology.

These categories help Aurex learn before it claims.

## 70. Stakeholder Discovery Before National Pitch

Before national-scale pitching, Aurex should progress through:

```text
VALIDATED PROBLEM
      |
      v
DEFINED USE CASE
      |
      v
EVIDENCE
      |
      v
TECHNICAL CAPABILITY
      |
      v
STAKEHOLDER VALUE
      |
      v
PROGRAMME PROPOSAL
```

A national pitch without this chain risks becoming narrative before proof.

## 71. Government-Ready Communication

When communicating with government or public institutions, Aurex should separate:

```text
VERIFIED FACTS
      |
      v
REASONABLE INFERENCES
      |
      v
AUREX HYPOTHESES
      |
      v
PROPOSED SOLUTIONS
      |
      v
LONG-TERM VISION
```

This allows public-sector stakeholders to understand what is established, what is inferred, what is proposed and what remains aspirational.

Government-ready communication should be accurate enough that Aurex can defend it later.

## 72. Partnership Maturity P0-P4

Aurex can use a simple partnership maturity model:

### P0 - No Partnership

No partnership exists.

### P1 - Exploratory Relationship

Discussion or discovery has occurred, but no material joint commitment exists.

### P2 - Defined Collaboration

A limited activity is defined, with responsibilities understood.

### P3 - Formal Partnership

A written agreement establishes responsibilities, scope and authority.

### P4 - Strategic Partnership

The relationship is long-term, material and institutionally important.

Aurex should not skip maturity levels in its public language.

---

## 73. Minimum Partnership Baseline - Now

At the current stage, a material partnership should not proceed without at least:

- a clear objective;
- accurate relationship label;
- authority check;
- contribution model;
- risk review;
- data and IP review where relevant;
- public-claims boundary;
- relationship owner;
- record in the appropriate register.

This is the minimum baseline, not the mature future system.

---

## 74. Partnership Decision Test

Before accepting or announcing a partnership, Aurex should ask:

```text
IS THE PROBLEM REAL?
IS THIS PARTNER RELEVANT?
IS THE AUTHORITY LEGITIMATE?
ARE THE TERMS CLEAR?
ARE CLAIMS ACCURATE?
ARE DATA AND IP PROTECTED?
ARE SAFETY AND SECURITY PRESERVED?
DOES THIS BUILD CAPABILITY?
CAN AUREX EXIT RESPONSIBLY?
```

If the answer to a material question is unknown, the relationship should remain exploratory until clarified.

## 75. Healthy Partnership Equation

A healthy Aurex partnership can be summarized as:

```text
MISSION FIT
+
LEGITIMATE AUTHORITY
+
DEFINED CONTRIBUTIONS
+
EVIDENCE-BASED VALUE
+
CLEAR TERMS
+
DATA / IP DISCIPLINE
+
SECURITY / SAFETY CONTROLS
+
PUBLIC-INTEREST ALIGNMENT
+
EXIT PATH
=
RESPONSIBLE PARTNERSHIP
```

If any element is missing, the relationship may still be useful, but it should not be overstated.

## 76. Ecosystem Principle

Aurex should build relationships as part of an ecosystem, not as a collection of badges.

The goal is not to appear connected.

The goal is to understand the energy-system problem, build credible capability, respect legitimate authority, and cooperate where cooperation helps African energy systems become more reliable, resilient, interoperable, secure, affordable and productive.
