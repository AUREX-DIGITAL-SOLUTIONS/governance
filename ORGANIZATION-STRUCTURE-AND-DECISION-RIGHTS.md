# Task 8 - Organization Structure & Decision Rights

## Aurex Digital Solutions

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Foundational Draft / Draft v1.0  
**Institution:** Aurex Digital Solutions  
**Current maturity:** Founder-led / pre-formalization  
**Purpose:** Define the institutional structure, roles, accountabilities, decision ownership, delegation, and authority boundaries of Aurex Digital Solutions.

**Navigation:** [Previous: Governance Framework](./GOVERNANCE-FRAMEWORK.md) | [Home: Documentation Index](./README.md) | [Next: Open-Source & Licensing Framework](./OPEN-SOURCE-AND-LICENSING-FRAMEWORK.md)

Task 7 established the governance architecture.

Task 8 answers:

> Who occupies which institutional role, what is each role accountable for, and exactly who has authority to make each class of decision?

The objective is not to create an impressive organization chart.

It is to create an institution that can actually operate.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines Aurex Digital Solutions' draft organization structure and decision-rights model for the founding stage.

It establishes:

- the difference between institutional roles and current staffing;
- the target functional architecture Aurex may grow toward;
- role accountabilities and authority boundaries;
- decision rights using the P/R/A/E/I model;
- reserved decisions;
- technical, financial, programme, security, access, and GitHub decision rights;
- role appointment and delegation registers;
- rules for creating future roles and organizational units.

This document does not create:

- a board that does not yet exist;
- employees who have not been appointed;
- committees that have not been constituted;
- legal powers Aurex has not acquired;
- authority to control third-party energy infrastructure.

---

## 2. Structural Principle

Aurex should separate three concepts:

```text
ROLE
What function exists?

ACCOUNTABILITY
What outcome is that function responsible for?

AUTHORITY
What may that function decide?
```

A title alone creates none of these.

Therefore:

> Every Aurex role must eventually have a defined purpose, accountability, authority boundary, and reporting relationship.

## 6. Organization Layers

Aurex should eventually operate through four structural layers.

### Layer 1 - Institutional Oversight

Protects:

- purpose;
- mission;
- institutional integrity;
- financial sustainability;
- public-interest obligations;
- major risk oversight.

Future mechanism:

**Governing Body**

Not yet constituted.

### Layer 2 - Executive Leadership

Responsible for running the institution.

```text
STRATEGY
   |
   v
RESOURCES
   |
   v
PROGRAMMES
   |
   v
OPERATIONS
   |
   v
RESULTS
```

Executive leadership is accountable for converting institutional strategy into execution.

### Layer 3 - Institutional Functions

Specialized capabilities required by Aurex.

Examples:

- Engineering & Technology;
- Research & Innovation;
- Programmes;
- Operations;
- Finance;
- Partnerships;
- Security;
- Risk;
- Legal/Compliance.

Functions should exist because work requires them, not because mature corporations have them.

### Layer 4 - Programmes and Projects

Where actual mission delivery happens.

```text
AUREX
  |
  +-- Programme A
  |      +-- Project
  |      +-- Project
  |
  +-- Programme B
  |
  +-- Future Programme
```

This allows Aurex to grow without reorganizing the institution around every new product.

A governing body should be created when institutional maturity justifies one.

Potential responsibilities:

- protect mission;
- oversee leadership;
- approve strategy;

## 16. Assurance Functions

Some functions must be capable of challenging delivery teams.

Potential assurance areas:

```text
SECURITY
SAFETY
RISK
LEGAL
ETHICS
RESEARCH INTEGRITY
FINANCIAL CONTROL
```

The key principle is:

> The team responsible for delivering an outcome should not always be the sole authority determining whether the outcome is safe, compliant, or ready.

At the current founder-led stage, assurance may rely on documented self-review, peer review, external advice, or staged approval until dedicated assurance roles exist.

## 17. Role Separation

Certain combinations of authority become dangerous as Aurex grows.

For example:

```text
REQUEST MONEY
     +
APPROVE MONEY
     +
RECEIVE MONEY
     +
RECONCILE MONEY
```

should eventually be separated.

Similarly:

```text
BUILD SYSTEM
     +
SECURITY-APPROVE SYSTEM
     +
DEPLOY SYSTEM
     +
AUDIT SYSTEM
```

should progressively gain independent review.

At the current founder stage, complete separation may be impossible.

Where separation cannot yet exist, Aurex should document the concentration of authority as a risk.

## 18. Decision Rights Model Using P/R/A/E/I

Aurex should adopt five decision roles.

### P - Propose

Develops the decision proposal.

### R - Review

Examines evidence, risks, constraints, and implications.

### A - Approve

Has authority to authorize the decision.

### E - Execute

Implements the approved decision.

### I - Inform

Must be informed of the decision.

This gives Aurex a P/R/A/E/I model.

It is more useful for institutional decisions than relying only on RACI.

## 21. Reserved Decisions

Certain decisions should remain at the highest governance level.

Potential reserved decisions include:

- changing institutional purpose;
- changing mission;
- amending core Charter provisions;
- merger;
- dissolution;
- transfer of institutional control;
- disposal of core institutional intellectual property;
- major borrowing;
- entry into a fundamentally different business;
- appointment or removal of top executive leadership.

At the current stage, these remain founder-reserved unless constrained by law, agreements, or adopted governance documents.

Future legal governance must supersede this transitional arrangement.

## 22. Technical Decision Rights

Routine technical decisions should be delegated as early as practical.

Example:

```text
ENGINEER
can choose:
implementation details

TECHNICAL LEAD
can choose:
component architecture

TECHNICAL GOVERNANCE
can approve:
major architectural change

EXECUTIVE / GOVERNANCE
decides:
material strategic or financial implications
```

Executives should not need to approve every line of code.

Likewise, engineers should not independently decide institutional strategy.

---

## 24. Financial Decision Rights

Financial authority should eventually use thresholds.

For example:

```text
LOW VALUE
-> delegated operational approval

MEDIUM VALUE
-> executive approval

HIGH VALUE
-> governing approval

EXTRAORDINARY
-> reserved decision
```

Do not invent monetary thresholds yet.

Task 16 defines financial controls based on actual finances, legal structure, funding conditions, and internal controls.

Until then, financial decisions should be documented, proportionate, and tied to legitimate institutional purpose.

---

## 25. Programme Decision Rights

Programme owners should control routine delivery within:

- approved objectives;
- budget;
- risk tolerance;
- architecture;
- timeline.

They should escalate when:

- scope materially changes;
- budget exceeds authority;
- major risk emerges;
- legal authority is unclear;
- safety is threatened;
- programme objectives become invalid.

---

## 26. Security Decision Rights

Security needs independent authority to stop unsafe technical actions.

A designated security authority should eventually be able to:

- block vulnerable releases;
- revoke compromised access;
- require remediation;
- coordinate incident response;
- require logging, audit, or access review;
- escalate material security risk.

Security should not be able to redesign institutional strategy simply because it controls security approvals.

Authority remains scoped.

---

## 27. Stop-Work Rights

Anyone may raise a stop-work request for credible safety or security concerns.

Authorized safety/security leadership determines continuation after assessment.

In an immediate hazard, personnel should take reasonable action consistent with applicable safety procedures.

No person should be punished for raising a genuine concern in good faith.

---

## 28. Access Rights Follow Roles

System permissions should derive from institutional responsibility.

```text
ROLE
 |
 v
REQUIRED CAPABILITY
 |
 v
MINIMUM ACCESS
 |
 v
AUDIT
```

Not:

```text
"I've always had admin."
```

As Aurex grows, privileged access should be periodically reviewed.

## 29. GitHub Decision Rights

This principle should apply directly to Aurex's open-source infrastructure.

Potential repository roles:

### Contributor

Can propose changes.

### Maintainer

Can review and merge within assigned scope.

### Technical Lead

Owns technical direction within defined project scope.

### Repository Administrator

Manages repository configuration and access.

### Organization Administrator

Manages organization-wide configuration.

These roles should not automatically be interchangeable.

Use least privilege.

## 33. Organizational Units

Aurex should create organizational units only when one of these becomes true:

1. meaningful recurring workload exists;
2. specialist accountability is necessary;
3. risk requires separation;
4. multiple people need coordination;
5. institutional scale requires dedicated leadership.

Therefore:

> Structure follows capability and workload, not ambition.

## 34. Programme vs Department

This distinction is important.

### Department/function

Provides enduring capability.

Example:

**Engineering**

### Programme

Pursues a defined mission outcome when authorized through governance.

Example:

**Facility EMS validation programme or future National Energy Operating System programme concept**

Therefore:

```text
ENGINEERING
can serve
      |
      v
AUTHORIZED PROGRAMME OR PROGRAMME CONCEPT
      +

      +
Future Programme B
```

Do not make NEOS the entire organizational structure.

## 37. Hiring / Role-Creation Trigger

A new dedicated role should generally be created when:

```text
WORKLOAD
   +
RISK
   +
ACCOUNTABILITY NEED
   +
AVAILABLE RESOURCES
   |
   v
JUSTIFIES ROLE
```

Not because:

> Organizations normally have a CTO.

This principle will keep Aurex lean.

## 38. Accountability Rule

Every material outcome should have one ultimately accountable role.

Multiple people may execute.

Multiple people may review.

But accountability should be identifiable.

```text
ONE ACCOUNTABLE OWNER
        +
MANY CONTRIBUTORS
```

---

## 39. No Permanent Founder Bottleneck

Aurex should deliberately measure whether routine work still requires founder approval.

If every:

- pull request;
- purchase;
- partnership email;
- research decision;
- architecture choice;
- publication;

requires the founder, the organization has not yet institutionalized.

Founder dependency should decrease as governance maturity increases.

---

## 40. Succession Principle

Aurex should eventually be able to answer:

> What happens if a key role holder is unavailable tomorrow?

Important roles should progressively have:

- documented responsibilities;
- accessible records;
- delegated backups;
- controlled credentials;
- handover procedures.

Institutional knowledge should not live solely inside one person's head.

---
