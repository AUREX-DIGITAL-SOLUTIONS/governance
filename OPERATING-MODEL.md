# Task 14 - Operating Model
## Aurex Digital Solutions

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Foundational Draft / Draft v1.0  
**Institution:** Aurex Digital Solutions  
**Phase:** Operations  
**Scope:** How Aurex converts institutional purpose into coordinated day-to-day work  
**Navigation:** [Previous: Risk Management Framework](./RISK-MANAGEMENT-FRAMEWORK.md) | [Home: Documentation Index](./README.md) | [Next: Organization Roadmap](./ORGANIZATION-ROADMAP.md)

Task 13 established how Aurex identifies and governs **uncertainty and risk**.

Task 14 establishes how Aurex actually **works**.

> **The Operating Model is the bridge between Aurex's institutional architecture and execution.**

Aurex does not yet need the bureaucracy of a mature corporation. It needs a lightweight operating system capable of taking the institution from founder-led formation through research, engineering, programmes, partnerships and eventually scaled operations.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Aurex Operating Model defines how:

- work enters the institution;
- priorities are established;
- responsibilities are assigned;
- research is conducted;
- technology is developed;
- programmes are created;
- decisions are made;
- risks are managed;
- information is documented;
- work is reviewed;
- institutional knowledge is retained;
- outcomes are measured.

The objective is:

```text
PURPOSE
   |
   v
STRATEGY
   |
   v
PRIORITIES
   |
   v
WORK
   |
   v
EVIDENCE
   |
   v
DECISIONS
   |
   v
OUTCOMES
   |
   v
LEARNING
```

## 2. Operating Principle

Aurex adopts:

> **Work should move through the institution by explicit purpose, accountable ownership, evidence and documented decisions.**

This prevents Aurex from becoming a collection of disconnected projects.

---

## 3. Institution Before Projects

Projects should serve Aurex.

Aurex should not exist merely to serve whichever project currently receives the most attention.

```text
AUREX DIGITAL SOLUTIONS
        |
        +-- Institutional Functions
        |
        +-- Research
        |
        +-- Engineering
        |
        +-- Programmes
              |
              +-- Projects
```

This preserves institutional continuity when individual projects begin, change or end.

---

## 4. Current Operating Reality

Aurex is currently founder-led.

Therefore, several functions may presently be performed by one person.

That is acceptable during formation.

It must simply be represented truthfully.

Current minimum model:

```text
FOUNDER
  |
  +-- Institution Building
  +-- Research
  +-- Engineering / Technical Development
  +-- Programme Development
  +-- External Engagement
```

This is **function concentration**, not evidence that five departments currently exist.

Aurex is moving from:

```text
O0 - FOUNDER-OPERATED
```

toward:

```text
O1 - DOCUMENTED
```

The immediate objective is not to pretend that mature institutional machinery already exists. The objective is to make the founder-led operating reality explicit, reviewable and capable of being delegated over time.

---

As Aurex matures, the operating model should evolve toward:

```text

          v
```

Functions should become formal only when workload, risk and accountability justify them.

These functions are future functional architecture, not current staffing claims.

## 6. Core Operating Functions

Aurex requires six broad functions over time:

1. **Institutional Direction**
2. **Research & Innovation**
3. **Engineering & Technology**
4. **Programme Development & Delivery**
5. **Operations & Administration**
6. **Assurance & Governance**

These are functions, not current departments, executives, committees or staffing claims.

---

## 7. Institutional Direction

This function maintains:

- purpose;
- mission;
- strategy;
- priorities;
- institutional architecture;
- major relationships;
- resource direction.

During formation, this responsibility primarily sits with the founder.

As Aurex matures, authority should become distributed through documented governance.

- security review;

- financial controls;
- legal review;
- technical assurance;
- programme evaluation.

At Aurex's current scale, assurance may be performed through structured review rather than dedicated staff.

## 13. How Work Enters Aurex

New work may originate from:

- institutional priorities;
- research findings;
- technical problems;
- stakeholder requests;
- programme needs;
- security issues;
- operational issues;
- partnership opportunities;
- contributor proposals.

All incoming work should not automatically become active work.

## 14. Work Intake

A lightweight intake should answer:

```text
WHAT IS BEING REQUESTED?
WHY DOES IT MATTER?
WHO BENEFITS?
HOW DOES IT SUPPORT THE MISSION?
WHAT EVIDENCE SUPPORTS IT?
WHAT RESOURCES ARE REQUIRED?
WHAT RISKS EXIST?
WHO WOULD OWN IT?
```

Then decide whether to proceed.

## 17. Priority

Priority should not be determined solely by excitement.


---

## 18. Priority Levels

A lightweight system:

### P0 - Critical

Immediate safety, security, legal or institutional-continuity issue.

### P1 - High

Material mission or programme priority.

### P2 - Normal

Important planned work.

### P3 - Opportunity

Useful but non-essential.

Priority should not become a mechanism for labeling everything urgent.

---

## 19. Work Ownership

Every active material item should have one accountable owner.

```text
ONE OUTCOME
    |
    v
ONE ACCOUNTABLE OWNER
```

Many people may contribute.

Accountability should still be identifiable.

One accountable owner per material outcome is a core operating requirement.

---

## 20. Definition of Done

Every material work item should define what completion means before significant execution begins.

Example:

For a research task:

> Evidence collected, sources documented, assumptions separated from verified facts, findings reviewed and conclusion recorded.

For software:

> Requirements satisfied, tests passed, documentation updated and required review completed.

Without a Definition of Done, work tends to remain indefinitely "almost finished."

---

## 21. Evidence-Based Progress

Aurex should distinguish:

```text
ACTIVITY
   !=
OUTPUT
   !=
OUTCOME
   !=
IMPACT
```

Example:

**Activity:** build Facility EMS integration.

**Output:** working integration.

**Outcome:** facility operator obtains reliable visibility.

**Impact:** improved energy-system performance or reliability.

The institution should increasingly measure outcomes rather than volume of activity.


Aurex should maintain institutional truth before narrative.

## 34. Meetings

Meetings should exist to:

- decide;
- coordinate;
- review;
- resolve;
- learn.

Not merely to demonstrate activity.

A useful rule:

> **If information can be communicated effectively asynchronously, a meeting may not be necessary.**

---

## 35. Meeting Outputs

Material meetings should result in at least one of:

- decision;
- action;
- owner;
- deadline;
- escalation;
- documented learning.

Otherwise the meeting may have created conversation rather than operational progress.

## 40. Human + AI Operating Model

AI can materially increase Aurex's execution capacity.

Potential uses include:

- research support;
- documentation;
- coding;
- analysis;
- issue triage;
- testing assistance;
- planning;
- knowledge retrieval.

But:

```text
AI EXECUTION CAPACITY
        !=
INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY
```

Human/institutional accountability remains.

AI execution capacity does not create institutional authority.

---

## 41. AI Work Validation

AI-generated work should be validated according to consequence.

For example:

```text
DRAFT DOCUMENT
-> human review

CODE
-> review + tests

RESEARCH CLAIM
-> source validation

ENERGY CONTROL LOGIC
-> engineering validation + safety process
```

Validation intensity increases with impact.

Validation rigor scales with consequence.

---

## 42. Automation

Repeatable low-risk processes should progressively be automated.

Examples:

- documentation checks;
- link validation;
- testing;
- builds;
- dependency scanning;
- reporting.

Automation should reduce repetitive work while preserving traceability.

Automation should not silently create approval, deployment or external-representation authority.

---

## 43. Repository Operating Model

Repositories should have clear purposes.

A mature repository should progressively identify:

- owner/maintainer;
- maturity;
- license;
- contribution rules;
- security reporting;
- documentation;
- release status.

Repositories should not become abandoned institutional assets without ownership.

A useful issue should answer:

- what is the problem;
- why it matters;
- expected outcome;
- evidence;
- owner/status where applicable.

Issue trackers should not become dumping grounds for every idea.

## 53. Government Engagement

Government-facing communication should distinguish:

```text
VERIFIED FACT
REASONABLE INFERENCE
AUREX HYPOTHESIS
PROPOSED SOLUTION
LONG-TERM VISION
```

This is particularly important when discussing concepts such as Aurex's Kenya **last-mile visibility gap** research framing.

Aurex should not convert its own problem framing into an alleged government finding without evidence.

Verified fact, reasonable inference, Aurex hypothesis, proposed solution and long-term vision should remain distinct in government-facing work.

KPIs should only exist where they support decisions.

- meeting count;
- document count;

## 56. Operational Review

Periodic review should ask:

1. What did we intend to achieve?
2. What happened?
3. What evidence exists?
4. What blocked progress?
5. What risks changed?
6. What did we learn?
7. What should change next?

This creates an institutional learning loop.

## 61. Minimum Tooling Principle

Aurex should keep tooling proportional to actual scale.

The principle is:

> **Use the smallest tool stack that preserves clarity, accountability, evidence, security and continuity.**

Tooling should help the institution operate. It should not create process theater, maintenance burden, or a false impression of maturity.

---

## 62. Founding-Stage Operating Stack

At the founding stage, a reasonable operating stack may include:

- Git repositories for institutional and technical documentation;
- issue tracking for actionable work;
- decision records for material decisions;
- lightweight project boards where needed;
- secure credential management;
- periodic review notes;
- controlled folders for external-facing records;
- link and documentation checks.

This is sufficient until volume, contributors, risk or external obligations justify more formal systems.

---

## 63. Minimum Operating Baseline - Now

Aurex's immediate operating baseline should be:

1. Maintain the institutional documentation index.
2. Keep completed frameworks linked and versioned.
3. Separate verified status from aspirations.
4. Track active work with owner, priority and state.
5. Document material decisions.
6. Review risks before material work advances.
7. Validate AI-assisted work according to consequence.
8. Preserve one authoritative institutional truth.

This baseline supports movement from O0 Founder-Operated to O1 Documented.

---

## 64. Operating Model Maturity

Aurex can use a simple operating maturity model:

| Level | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| O0 | Founder-Operated | Work depends primarily on the founder's memory, judgment and direct execution. |
| O1 | Documented | Core purpose, status, decisions, risks and operating rules are written down and linked. |
| O2 | Repeatable | Common workflows are used consistently across institutional, research, engineering and programme work. |
| O3 | Delegated | Work can be owned by defined roles with appropriate authority, review and escalation. |
| O4 | Scaled | Multiple teams/functions operate through mature systems, assurance, reporting and governance. |

Aurex is currently moving from **O0 Founder-Operated** toward **O1 Documented**.

---

## 65. What Aurex Should Not Build Yet

Aurex should not prematurely create:

- fictional departments;
- executive titles without real authority and accountability;
- committees without defined decision rights;
- mature enterprise tooling without operational volume;
- production operations claims without support capacity;
- partnership claims without documented agreement;
- government endorsement claims without evidence;
- approval workflows that nobody can legitimately operate;
- pilots, deployments or institutional maturity that do not yet exist.

Stage awareness protects credibility.

## 68. What Task 15 Must Solve

Task 14 defines how Aurex should work.

Task 15 must define how Aurex moves from its current founding-stage reality toward a more capable institution over time.

Therefore the next artifact is:

**Task 15 - Organization Roadmap**

It should answer:

- what must be built first;
- what should wait;
- what maturity milestones matter;
- how founder dependency is reduced;
- how roles become formal;
